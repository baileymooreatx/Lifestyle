# Alcohol

Alcohol slows overall metabolism and disrupts several core metabolic pathways, 
which is why chronic drinking is tied to weight gain, insulin resistance, fatty 
liver, and broader disease.

The main mechanisms:

- **The liver prioritizes alcohol as a toxin.** It can't be stored, so the liver
  burns it first (roughly one standard drink/hour). While that's happening, fat
  oxidation and other nutrient processing are pushed aside. Fat burning can
  drop by up to ~50%, so excess calories from food get stored as fat instead of
  burned.
- **It disrupts cellular energy (NAD⁺/NADH).** Converting ethanol consumes NAD⁺
  and floods cells with NADH, which blocks glycolysis, the TCA cycle, and
  gluconeogenesis — effectively choking the mitochondria's ability to make ATP.
  This also impairs blood-sugar regulation and can cause hypoglycemia.
- **It drives oxidative stress and inflammation.** The toxic byproduct
  acetaldehyde damages mitochondria and proteins, and alcohol triggers reactive
  oxygen species (ROS), causing mitochondrial dysfunction across the liver,
  heart, pancreas, and brain.
- **It harms the gut and nutrient absorption.** Alcohol breaks down the
  intestinal barrier, alters the microbiome, and reduces uptake of key
  nutrients (thiamine, B12, folate, zinc, magnesium), which further impairs
  metabolic and immune function.
- **It throws off hormones and sleep.** It lowers growth hormone, testosterone,
  and other metabolic hormones, disrupts sleep, and increases cravings for
  calorie-dense food — all of which promote visceral fat and insulin resistance.

**The cumulative result** is a shift toward fat storage (especially
visceral/abdominal and liver fat), reduced muscle protein synthesis, impaired
glucose control, and chronic inflammation — the foundation for **metabolic
syndrome, type 2 diabetes, fatty liver disease, cardiovascular disease, and
certain cancers**.

## How Alcohol Sabotages Metabolism — A Deeper Look

### 1. The Redox Crisis: NAD⁺ Depletion and Mitochondrial Choking

This is the single most important mechanism. Ethanol oxidation happens in two
steps, each consuming NAD⁺ and producing NADH:

- **Step 1 (cytosol):** Alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH) converts ethanol →
  acetaldehyde + NADH
- **Step 2 (mitochondria):** Aldehyde dehydrogenase 2 (ALDH2) converts
  acetaldehyde → acetate + **another** NADH

The result is a massive spike in the intracellular **NADH/NAD⁺ ratio**. Because
NADH must be shuttled into the mitochondria via malate-aspartate and
glycerol-3-phosphate shuttles, and the electron transport chain (ETC) has finite
capacity, the excess NADH backs up and:

- **Inhibits the TCA cycle** — α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase and isocitrate
  dehydrogenase are both NADH-sensitive, so the cycle stalls
- **Blocks β-oxidation** — fatty acids can't be oxidized because the enzymes
  that feed them into the cycle are inhibited. This is why fat accumulates in
  the liver (steatosis) even before cirrhosis
- **Impairs gluconeogenesis** — the high NADH drives lactate → pyruvate and
  malate → oxaloacetate in the *wrong* direction, depleting the substrates
  needed to make glucose. This is the biochemical basis of **alcohol-induced
  hypoglycemia**, which is why a hungover, fasting person can crash
- **Suppresses SIRT1** — sirtuin 1 is an NAD⁺-dependent deacetylase that
  regulates PGC-1α, the master regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis. With NAD⁺
  depleted, SIRT1 activity drops, PGC-1α stays hyperacetylated and inactive, and
  **the cell loses its ability to make new mitochondria** (Frontiers in
  Toxicology, 2025)

The downstream effect: fewer, less functional mitochondria → less ATP → a cell
that is chronically energy-starved despite being "fed."

### 2. Oxidative Stress: A Self-Amplifying Damage Loop

The excess NADH doesn't just stall metabolism — it actively generates reactive
oxygen species (ROS):

- The ETC, overloaded with electrons, "leaks" them at **Complex I and Complex
  III**, reducing O₂ to superoxide (O₂•⁻) instead of passing them to cytochrome
  c
- Chronic alcohol also **induces CYP2E1**, a microsomal enzyme that shunts more
  electrons into the ROS pathway
- Acetaldehyde itself is a potent electrophile that **binds covalently to
  proteins** (forming adducts), mitochondrial DNA, and microtubules,
  structurally damaging them

The damage is not limited to the liver. Acetaldehyde-induced oxidative stress
has been documented in:

| **Organ**            | **Mechanism**                                                                                                                  |
|:---------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Liver                | Lipid peroxidation, mitochondrial depolarization, hepatocyte apoptosis                                                         |
| Heart                | Cardiomyocyte oxidative stress → alcoholic cardiomyopathy; acetaldehyde adducts in sarcolemmal membranes disrupt Ca²⁺ handling |
| Pancreas             | Mitochondrial permeability transition pore (MPTP) opening → ATP collapse → acinar cell death (pancreatitis)                    |
| Brain                | Neuronal oxidative damage, particularly in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex                                               |
| Vascular endothelium | DNA damage, impaired NO signaling, Ca²⁺ dysregulation → atherosclerosis and hypertension                                       |

Critically, oxidative stress and inflammation form a **vicious cycle**: ROS
activates NF-κB, which upregulates more CYP2E1 and more inflammatory cytokines (
IL-6, TNF-α), which generate more ROS. This is why the damage is progressive,
not just cumulative.

### 3. The Gut–Liver Axis: Dysbiosis, Leaky Gut, and Endotoxemia

This is arguably the most underappreciated pathway. Alcohol doesn't just damage
the gut lining — it **rewires the microbiome** in a way that feeds back into
liver and systemic disease:

- **Dysbiosis:** Chronic alcohol decreases SCFA-producing bacteria (which
  produce butyrate, a key fuel for colonocytes and a barrier-strengthening
  signal) and selects for ethanol-tolerant, pro-inflammatory species. A 2026
  study in *Frontiers in Microbiology* showed that **12 months** of chronic
  alcohol use in humans significantly reduced SCFA levels and shifted the fecal
  metabolome toward fatty acid oxidation and tyrosine metabolism pathways
- **Barrier breakdown:** Ethanol and acetaldehyde directly damage **tight
  junction proteins** (claudin-1, occludin, ZO-1, E-cadherin). This happens
  within hours of a single heavy drinking episode. The result is increased
  intestinal permeability — "leaky gut"
- **Endotoxemia:** With the barrier compromised, **lipopolysaccharide (LPS)**
  from gram-negative gut bacteria translocates into the portal circulation. LPS
  activates **Kupffer cells** (resident liver macrophages) via TLR4, triggering
  a massive release of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6
- **The gut–liver–brain axis:** This inflammatory cascade doesn't stop at the
  liver. The same cytokines and microbial metabolites that reach the liver also
  cross into systemic circulation and the brain, contributing to the
  neuroinflammation seen in alcohol use disorder

The clinical significance: this is why even "moderate" drinking is associated
with increased LPS levels in serum, and why the gut microbiome is now considered
a **druggable target** in alcohol-associated liver disease.

### 4. Nutrient Depletion — A Multiplier

Alcohol doesn't just add empty calories; it actively **steals nutrients**
the body needs to run metabolism.

- **Thiamine (B1):** ADH and the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex both require
  thiamine. Alcohol impairs intestinal absorption and increases renal excretion.
  Depletion → impaired pyruvate → acetyl-CoA conversion → Wernicke-Korsakoff
  syndrome (brain damage)
- **Magnesium:** Alcohol is a diuretic that flushes Mg²⁺ through the kidneys
  while simultaneously reducing gut absorption. Low Mg²⁺ impairs over 300
  enzymatic reactions, including those in ATP synthesis and insulin signaling
- **Folate and B12:** Reduced absorption → impaired one-carbon metabolism →
  elevated homocysteine (an independent cardiovascular risk factor) and impaired
  DNA methylation
- **Zinc:** Depleted by alcohol → impaired insulin signaling and immune function
- **Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K):** Alcohol inhibits their micelle
  formation and absorption in the gut

The net effect is a body trying to run on a **metabolically toxic substrate
while being starved of the cofactors** it needs to process that substrate and
everything else.

### 5. Hormonal and Epigenetic Disruption

- **Epigenetics:** The NADH/NAD⁺ shift inhibits SIRT1 (a histone deacetylase),
  altering chromatin structure and gene expression. Alcohol also depletes
  S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), a universal methyl donor, leading to **DNA
  hypomethylation** — a hallmark of cancer
- **Hormones:** Alcohol suppresses growth hormone (reducing muscle protein
  synthesis and lipolysis), lowers testosterone, and disrupts the HPA axis. It
  also impairs insulin receptor signaling, contributing to the **insulin
  resistance** that precedes type 2 diabetes
- **Sleep architecture:** Even moderate alcohol suppresses REM sleep and
  fragments deep sleep, which independently impairs glucose tolerance, increases
  ghrelin (hunger hormone), and reduces leptin (satiety hormone)

### 6. The Downstream Disease Landscape

All of the above converge on a well-characterized clinical progression:

> **Steatosis (fatty liver) → Steatohepatitis (MASH) → Fibrosis → Cirrhosis →
HCC (hepatocellular carcinoma)**

But the damage is systemic. The same oxidative stress, inflammation, and
metabolic derangement drive:

- **Metabolic syndrome** (visceral obesity, insulin resistance, dyslipidemia,
  hypertension)
- **Type 2 diabetes** (impaired β-cell function + peripheral insulin resistance)
- **Cardiovascular disease** (alcoholic cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias,
  atherosclerosis, stroke)
- **Pancreatitis** (acute and chronic)
- **Cancer** (esophageal, oral, colorectal, liver, breast — alcohol is a Group 1
  carcinogen per IARC)
- **Neurodegeneration** (cerebral atrophy, increased dementia risk)

### The Key Takeaway

Alcohol is metabolically toxic not because of its calories (7 kcal/g, second
only to fat), but because it **hijacks the redox machinery** that every cell
depends on. The NAD⁺ depletion → mitochondrial dysfunction → ROS →
inflammation → tissue damage cascade is the unifying thread, and it operates at
every level — from the single hepatocyte to the whole gut–liver–brain axis. This
is why the damage is **progressive, multi-organ, and cumulative**, and why "just
one drink" still initiates the same biochemical cascade, just at lower
amplitude.
