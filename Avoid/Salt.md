# Salt

The core problem is **high blood pressure (hypertension)**. Sodium attracts
water, so excess salt pulls fluid into your bloodstream, increasing blood volume
and the pressure on your vessel walls. Over time, this extra strain stiffens
arteries and forces the heart to work harder, leading to **heart disease, heart
attack, stroke, and heart failure**.

Beyond the cardiovascular system, too much salt:

- **Strains the kidneys**, which must excrete the excess, potentially causing
  fluid buildup, kidney disease, and stones.
- **Raises stomach cancer risk** — salt may damage the stomach lining and
  interact with *Helicobacter pylori*; a 2025 study linked ~8% of global gastric
  cancer deaths to high sodium intake.
- **Leaches calcium from bones**, increasing osteoporosis risk.
- **Promotes inflammation** via effects on the immune system and gut bacteria.

Short-term, excess salt causes bloating, swelling, thirst, and poor sleep.

The average American consumes **~3,400 mg of sodium per day**, well above the
recommended limit of 2,300 mg (and 1,500 mg for those with hypertension).
Roughly 75–80% of that comes from packaged and restaurant foods, not the salt
shaker.

## How Salt Raises Blood Pressure  

The simple "salt holds water" story is only part of it. The process is actually
**multi-pathway**.

1. **Osmotic fluid retention.** Excess sodium in the blood draws water out of
   cells and into the bloodstream by osmosis, expanding blood volume. The heart
   must pump more fluid with each beat, raising pressure.
2. **Vascular stiffening.** Chronic volume overload damages the endothelium
   (inner lining of arteries), making vessels stiffer and less able to dilate.
   Peripheral resistance rises, compounding the pressure increase.
3. **Hormonal amplification.** High sodium stimulates the
   **renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS)** and increases sympathetic
   nervous system outflow. Angiotensin II is one of the most potent
   vasoconstrictors known, so strong that two entire classes of blood pressure
   drugs target it.
4. **Immune activation.** A 2019 *Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine* review
   confirmed that sodium directly modulates immune cell function. High-salt
   microenvironments in tissues cause local inflammation and T-helper cell
   (Th17) activation, which independently drives vascular damage and
   hypertension.

Not everyone responds equally. **Salt sensitivity** varies by genetics, age, and
ethnicity. African Americans, older adults, and people with existing
hypertension tend to be far more sensitive.

## Kidney Strain

Your kidneys are the primary sodium-excretion organ. When they're overwhelmed:

- They must work harder to filter excess sodium, pulling more water with it
  (osmosis).
- Over time, the constant high-pressure filtration **damages the glomeruli**,
  the kidney's filtering units, accelerating chronic kidney disease.
- The resulting fluid retention feeds back into higher blood pressure, creating
  a **vicious cycle** between kidney damage and hypertension.

## Stomach Cancer — Stronger Than Most People Realize

The evidence here is robust and dose-dependent.

- A meta-analysis of prospective studies found **68% higher risk** of gastric
  cancer with high salt intake and **41% higher** with moderate intake, compared
  to low intake.
- A 2024 UK Biobank study (~500,000 participants) found that people who **always
  added table salt** to food had a **41% greater risk** of gastric cancer than
  those who rarely or never did.
- The Stomach cancer Pooling (StoP) Project pooled analysis (2022) confirmed 
  that salty taste preference, always using table salt, and high intake of 
  salt-preserved foods each independently increased risk.

**Mechanism:** Salt directly damages the gastric mucosa (stomach lining),
causing chronic inflammation and erosion. It also **potentiates *Helicobacter 
pylori*** infection, the bacterium responsible for most gastric cancers, by 
increasing its virulence and making the stomach lining more vulnerable. The 
combination of **physical damage and bacterial synergy** is what drives the 
cancer risk.

## Bone Loss — The Calcium Drain

This is a less-discussed but well-supported effect.

- High sodium intake **increases urinary calcium excretion** (calciuria). For
  every 1 gram of excess sodium, the body excretes roughly 24 to 42 mg of
  additional calcium.
- This creates a **negative calcium balance**: more calcium leaves the body than
  comes in, so the body compensates by pulling calcium from bone.
- A 2025 systematic review in *Frontiers in Endocrinology* confirmed that
  excessive salt correlates with elevated urinary calcium and contributes to 
  **measurable bone mineral density loss** in postmenopausal women.
- At the cellular level, sodium loading **uncouples bone remodeling**. It
  enhances osteoclast activity (bone breakdown), while reducing osteoblast
  activity (bone formation), accelerating skeletal deterioration.
- Paradoxically, low-sodium diets that activate RAAS can *also* impair bone,
  because RAAS components in bone tissue stimulate osteoclast formation and
  inhibit osteoblasts.

## Gut Microbiome — The New Frontier

Research from 2024 to 2026 has revealed that salt's effects extend well beyond 
the cardiovascular system through the **gut-brain-immune axis**.

- High-salt diets **deplete *Lactobacillus*** and other beneficial bacteria that
  produce [short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs)](../Body/ShortChainFattyAcid.md), 
  which normally keep inflammation in check.
- The loss of SCFAs removes a brake on **Th17 inflammatory cells**, which then
  migrate systemically and drive vascular inflammation and blood pressure
  elevation.
- Excess salt also **disrupts the intestinal barrier** (increases gut
  permeability, or "leaky gut"), allowing microbial products like
  lipopolysaccharides (LPS) to enter the bloodstream and trigger **systemic
  low-grade inflammation**.
- A 2026 study in *Frontiers in Microbiology* showed that high-salt diets in
  salt-sensitive rats elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines and caused 
  **structural damage to the colon**.
- A 2026 mouse study found that high-salt diets not only disrupted the
  microbiome but also **impaired memory**, likely through inflammation-mediated
  damage to the hippocampus.

## Who's Most at Risk

| **Group**                                           | **Why**                                                                                                 |
|:----------------------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| African Americans                                   | Higher prevalence of salt-sensitive hypertension; ~60% are salt-sensitive vs. ~40% of white populations |
| Older adults (60+)                                  | Kidney function declines, reducing sodium excretion capacity                                            |
| People with existing hypertension or kidney disease | Already-compensated systems are pushed past their limit                                                 |
| Those with *H. pylori* infection                    | Salt synergizes with the bacterium to raise gastric cancer risk                                         |
| Postmenopausal women                                | Already losing calcium via estrogen decline; salt accelerates the loss                                  |

## Summary  

Salt isn't just a "blood pressure problem." It's a **systemic disruptor** that
simultaneously strains the heart, kidneys, bones, gut, immune system, and
stomach lining. The effects compound: the inflammation from gut dysbiosis
worsens vascular damage, the vascular damage worsens kidney function, the kidney
damage worsens blood pressure. Breaking the cycle at any point helps, but
reducing sodium intake addresses the upstream driver of most of these cascades.
