# Immune System of the Central Nervous System

The central nervous system (CNS) has its own dedicated immune system, long
called "immune-privileged" but now understood as a **precisely regulated
immunological interface** rather than an isolated one.

## Key Immune Cells of the CNS

- **[Microglia](Microglia.md)** are the brain's resident 
  [macrophages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrophage), making up ~80% of 
  brain immune cells. They continuously survey the 
  [parenchyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parenchyma), detect pathogens via
  [toll-like receptors (TLRs)](Troll-LikeReceptor.md), and can shift between
  neuroprotective and pro-inflammatory phenotypes.
- **[Astrocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrocyte)** also participate in 
  innate defense, upregulating inflammatory processes in response to injury or 
  infection.
- **Meningeal immune cells** — [T cells](T-Cell.md), [B cells](B-Cell.md),
  natural killer cells, and type 2 innate lymphoid cells (ILC2) — reside in the 
  [dura mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dura_mater) and 
  [meninges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meninges), monitoring cerebrospinal 
  fluid for signs of infection, tumor, or injury.

## Barriers and Drainage  

The **blood-brain barrier (BBB)** restricts free passage of immune cells into
the brain parenchyma, creating a low-baseline inflammatory environment that
protects delicate, non-regenerating neurons. However, recent discoveries of 
**meningeal lymphatic vessels** and the **glymphatic system** have shown 
that the brain actively drains immune signals to cervical lymph nodes, enabling 
immune surveillance from the outside. 
The [choroid plexus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choroid_plexus) also hosts 
immune cells that orchestrate selective homing into the CNS.

## How It Works

- **Innate immunity** (microglia, astrocytes) is the first line of defense,
  responding to pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) and damage
  signals via [TLR](Troll-LikeReceptor.md) and [NLR](NOD-LikeReceptor.md) 
  pathways.
- **Adaptive immunity** (T cells, B cells) is slower but more specific. When the
  BBB is breached or inflammation is triggered, peripheral immune cells are
  recruited in.
- **[Cytokines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytokine)** (IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α, 
  IFN-γ, IL-10, TGF-β) are the critical signaling molecules that coordinate both 
  protective and potentially damaging responses.

## Bidirectional Crosstalk  

The CNS and immune system actively regulate each other. Neurons modulate
microglia and immune responses through neurotransmitters and hormones (e.g., via
the [hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamic%E2%80%93pituitary%E2%80%93adrenal_axis) 
and sympathetic nervous system). Conversely, immune-derived cytokines from the 
meninges influence brain function, including cognition and mood.

## Clinical Relevance  

Dysregulation of CNS immunity underlies diseases like **multiple sclerosis**
(where T cells attack myelin), **Alzheimer's disease** (chronic microglial
activation), and various CNS infections. The goal of current research is to
learn how to modulate these immune checkpoints, suppressing autoimmunity while
preserving protective immunity.
