# Propionate

Propionate is a three-carbon 
[short-chain fatty acid (SCFA)](./ShortChainFattyAcid.md) produced primarily by 
gut microbial fermentation of dietary fiber. Its metabolic functions are 
distinct from other SCFAs, particularly acetate, and span energy production, 
glucose homeostasis, lipid regulation, and signaling.

## Core Metabolic Pathway

Propionate is absorbed (~90% from the colon) and transported via the portal vein
to the liver, where it is activated to **propionyl-CoA** by acyl-CoA synthetase
3 (ACSS3). The primary fate is a multistep anaplerotic pathway:

**Propionyl-CoA → D-methylmalonyl-CoA → L-methylmalonyl-CoA → succinyl-CoA**
(via B₁₂-dependent methylmalonyl-CoA mutase)

This is the **only SCFA pathway that enables tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle 
anaplerosis**, replenishing cycle intermediates when they are depleted.

## Key Metabolic Functions

| **Function**                 | **Mechanism**                                                                                                                                                |
|:-----------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Gluconeogenesis              | Succinyl-CoA → oxaloacetate → glucose; propionate is a major substrate for hepatic glucose production                                                        |
| Lipid/cholesterol regulation | Competitively inhibits acetate uptake by hepatocytes, reducing hepatic lipogenesis and cholesterol biosynthesis; lowers serum cholesterol                    |
| Energy & body weight         | Reduces adiposity and body weight; promotes adipose tissue "browning" (↑UCP1, ↑PGC1α) and thermogenesis                                                      |
| Appetite/satiety signaling   | Activates GPR41 (FFAR3) and GPR43 (FFAR2) on enteroendocrine cells → stimulates **PYY** and **GLP-1** release → reduces appetite and energy intake           |
| Epigenetic regulation        | Inhibits histone deacetylases (HDAC8/9), increasing histone acetylation and propionylation; modulates gene expression in immune, neuronal, and cardiac cells |
| Direct anabolic condensation | Two propionyl-CoA molecules can condense to form *trans*-2-methyl-pentenoyl-CoA (a 6-carbon unit), a pathway recently confirmed in vivo in human myocardium  |
| Immune modulation            | Dampens NF-κB, IL-6, STAT1/STAT3 signaling; augments Treg/Th17 ratio; anti-inflammatory in gut and systemic tissues                                          |
| Urea cycle interaction       | Propionyl-CoA can deplete hepatic acetyl-CoA, inhibiting N-acetylglutamate synthesis and thus carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I (first urea cycle step)       |

## Sources of Propionate

- Gut microbial fermentation (mainly *Bacteroides*, *Alistipes*, *Coprococcus*)
- Catabolism of odd-chain fatty acids
- Catabolism of branched-chain amino acids (isoleucine, valine), methionine, and
  threonine
- Cholesterol side-chain oxidation

## Clinical Note

Inborn errors of propionate metabolism (e.g., **propionic acidemia**,
methylmalonyl-CoA mutase deficiency) cause toxic accumulation of propionyl-CoA,
metabolic acidosis, and hyperammonemia, underscoring how central this pathway is
to systemic homeostasis.

## Foods that Increase Propionate Production

The key is targeting the specific fiber types that propionate-producing
bacteria (*Bacteroides*, *Prevotella*, *Akkermansia*, *Dialister*,
*Phascolarctobacterium*) prefer to ferment.

### Top Fiber Types for Propionate

| **Fiber**                                                          | **Best Food Sources**                                                                         | **Why It Works**                                                                                                              |
|:-------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| [Inulin](../Supplements/Inulin.md) / Fructo-oligosaccharides (FOS) | Chicory root, Jerusalem artichoke, garlic, onions, leeks, asparagus, agave                    | Most direct propionate driver; a 50:50 inulin + arabinoxylan blend produced sustained propionate in continuous colonic models |
| [Resistant starch](../Food/OtherFoods/ResistantStarch.md)          | Cooked-and-cooled potatoes, rice, pasta; green (unripe) bananas; legumes (lentils, chickpeas) | Slow, distal fermentation; strongly correlated with *Bacteroidaceae* and propionate in humanized models                       |
| Pectin                                                             | Apples (skin on), pears, citrus, berries, okra                                                | Feeds *Bacteroides* via the succinate pathway; high propionate yield                                                          |
| β-Glucans                                                          | Oats, barley, algae (laminarin)                                                               | Shown *in vitro* to increase propionate proportionally to chain structure                                                     |
| Arabinoxylan                                                       | Wheat bran, barley                                                                            | Part of the most effective propionate-producing prebiotic blend studied                                                       |
| **Xylo-oligosaccharides / polydextrose**                           | Found in some whole grains; available as supplements                                          | Among the greatest propionate increases in batch fermentation studies                                                         |

### Summary

- **Highest-impact single foods:** Jerusalem artichoke (~10–22% inulin by
  weight), chicory root, cooked-and-cooled potatoes, green bananas, oats, and
  apples with skin.
- **Legumes** ([beans](../Food/Pulses/Pulses.md), 
  [lentils](../Food/Pulses/Lentil.md), [chickpeas](../Food/Pulses/Chickpea.md)) 
  are particularly effective because they combine 
  [resistant starch](../Food/OtherFoods/ResistantStarch.md), inulin-type 
  fructans, and pectin in one food.
- **Cooking tip:** Boil potatoes/rice/pasta, then **cool for at least 12 
  hours** before eating. This converts digestible starch to resistant starch 
  (RS3), roughly doubling the fermentable substrate that reaches the colon.
- **Fermented foods** ([yogurt](../Food/Fermented/Yogurt.md), 
  [kefir](../Food/Fermented/Kefir.md), sauerkraut) can support microbial
  diversity and cross-feeding, but they don't directly supply large amounts of
  propionate.

The overall principle: **diversity of fiber types** matters more than any single
food, because different substrates feed different propionate-producing genera
via the succinate vs. propanediol pathways.
