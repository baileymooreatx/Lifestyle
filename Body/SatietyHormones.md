# Satiety hormones

Satiety hormones are chemical messengers released by the gut, fat cells, and
pancreas that signal the brain (primarily the hypothalamus) to reduce appetite
and stop eating. They work in opposition to **ghrelin**, the main hunger
hormone.

| **Hormone**                     | **Source**                                        | **Timing**                   | **Key Action**                                                                                 |
|:--------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| CCK (cholecystokinin)           | Duodenum & jejunum                                | Acute (minutes after eating) | Slows gastric emptying, signals fullness in response to fat and protein                        |
| GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) | Intestinal L-cells (distal small intestine/colon) | Acute–moderate               | Delays gastric emptying, acts on hypothalamus/brainstem to reduce food intake                  |
| PYY (peptide YY)                | Intestinal L-cells (ileum/colon)                  | Acute–moderate               | Inhibits hunger neurons, delays stomach emptying; strongly stimulated by protein               |
| Insulin                         | Pancreas                                          | Acute                        | Crosses into the brain to dampen appetite after blood glucose rises                            |
| Leptin                          | Adipose (fat) cells                               | Long-term (days/weeks)       | Reflects total energy stores; tells the brain to reduce hunger and increase energy expenditure |

## How They Work Together

- **Short-term (meal-by-meal):** CCK, GLP-1, PYY, and insulin spike within
  minutes of eating, telling the brain to slow down and stop. It takes roughly 
  **20 minutes** for these hormonal signals to fully register.
- **Long-term:** Leptin provides a sustained signal about overall fat stores.
  High leptin should suppress appetite, but **leptin resistance** (common in
  obesity) blunts this signal.

## What Influences Them

- **Protein and fiber** are the most satiating macronutrients because they
  strongly stimulate GLP-1, PYY, and CCK release.
- **Sleep deprivation** raises ghrelin ~15% and lowers leptin ~15%, increasing
  hunger.
- **Weight loss** tends to increase ghrelin and temporarily lower PYY and GLP-1,
  which is why appetite rebounds after dieting.
- **Chronic stress** elevates cortisol, which can increase appetite for
  energy-dense foods.

GLP-1 is also the target of a class of weight-loss drugs (e.g., semaglutide,
tirzepatide) that mimic or prolong its satiety effects.
