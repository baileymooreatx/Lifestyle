# Fasting

Fasting promotes autophagy.

## [Indicators of Autophagy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K3F3KRaJeLE)

Autophagy onset occurs 17 to 24 hours after fast begins.
Deeper cellular cleaning requires 48 to 72 hours.

* Mental clarity from burning ketones instead of glucose
* Mild fatigue, but not wiped out
* Appetite comes and goes in 20-minute cycles
* Signs of ketosis
    * Fruity or metallic taste in your mouth
    * Reduced Appetite
    * Increased thirst
    * Mild fatigue or brain fog

## [Intermittent Fasting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kHomeCqyWKk)
Reduce the number of hours when you eat per day by one hour
each day until the window reaches eight hours or less.  
Meals should be about four hours apart with **no snacking** in between.

### [Fasting length](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f4idT8Mnj78)
1. [Prepare for fasting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnIrkwke_M8), take the following steps
    1. Cut out carbs 7 days before fast. Go in as a fat burner not a sugar burner.
    2. Eat three meals per day with no snacking between meals.
    3. Consume 2 tbsp each of avocado oil, [coconut oil](./Fats/CoconutOil.md) 
       and butter for 7 days leading up to fast.
2. 12 hour fast should be baseline.  
   Do not eat three to five hours before sleeping.  
   Improves insulin signaling.  
   Minimal fat loss.
3. 16 hour fast with an 8-hour eating window  
   Improves insulin sensitivity.  
   Burns fat, but only short term because it creates a cortisol adaptation.
4. 24 hour fast, also called one meal a day (OMAD) is optimal for fat loss, but will plateau.  
   Significant insulin drop  
   Growth hormone spikes  
   Increases autophagy  
   Deep ketosis
5. 36 hour fast  
   Deep ketosis  
   Deep autophagy
6. Mix up the schedule to prevent adaptation and plateaus.  
   Don't fast too much for too long. Have recovery days with no fasting.

| Difficulty   | Schedule                                                                                            |  
|:-------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|  
| Beginner     | 16 hour fast four days per week. Avoid fasting one day per week. 18/6 is a better variation.        | 
| Intermediate | 24 hour fast once per week. 12 to 14 hour eating window on other days                               |
| Advanced     | 36 hour fast once per week with a high protein re-feed day. Strength training on high protien days. |

## Fasting-Mimicking Diet

The Fasting-Mimicking Diet (FMD) is a **5-day, plant-based, low-calorie
protocol** developed by longevity researcher Valter Longo (director of the USC 
Longevity Institute) to trigger the metabolic benefits of a water fast while
still allowing some food.

### How It Works

By keeping calories, protein, and sugar very low, the FMD **downregulates
nutrient-sensing pathways** (insulin/IGF-1 and mTOR), which signals the body to
shift from "growth mode" to "repair mode." This triggers **autophagy** (cellular
cleanup), promotes **stem-cell regeneration**, and nudges the body into 
**fat-burning / mild ketosis**.

### Typical 5-Day Protocol

| Day              |     Calories | Protein | Fat | Carbs |
|:-----------------|-------------:|--------:|----:|------:|
| Day 1            |  ~1,100 kcal |     11% | 46% |   43% |
| Days 2 through 5 |    ~725 kcal |      9% | 44% |   47% |

### What You Eat

- **Allowed:** non-starchy vegetables (celery, zucchini, leafy greens), berries,
  small amounts of nuts/seeds, olive oil, avocado
- **Avoided:** refined carbs, animal proteins, dairy, high-glycemic foods,
  processed foods

The diet is typically repeated in **cycles** — e.g., 5 days once a month for 2–3
months — followed by normal eating. A commercial version is sold as **ProLon**
(formulated bars, soups, crackers).

### Claimed Benefits

Small human trials show improvements in **insulin sensitivity, blood pressure,
IGF-1 levels, body fat, and biological-age markers**. Animal studies suggest
potential for reducing inflammation and amyloid-beta accumulation (relevant to
Alzheimer's research).

### Caveats

- **Not suitable** for people trying to build or maintain muscle (very low 
  protein) or those with chronic conditions on medication without medical 
  supervision.
- More robust, large-scale human trials are still needed to fully validate
  long-term safety and effectiveness.
