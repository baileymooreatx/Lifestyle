# Kefir

Kefir is a fermented milk drink that is rich in probiotics, protein, calcium,
and B vitamins. Water kefir feeds on sugar solution instead of milk. This 
discussion only covers milk kefir.   

## Nutritional Profile  

One cup (8 oz) of low-fat milk kefir provides approximately:  

| **Nutrient**    | **Amount** | **% Daily Value** |
|:----------------|-----------:|------------------:|
| Calories        |        104 |                   |
| Protein         |        9 g |              ~18% |
| Calcium         |     316 mg |              ~24% |
| Phosphorus      |     255 mg |              ~20% |
| Vitamin B12     |    0.71 µg |              ~29% |
| Riboflavin (B2) |    0.33 mg |              ~25% |
| Vitamin D       |     100 IU |              ~12% |
| Potassium       |     400 mg |               ~8% |

Based on a 2,000-calorie diet
([USDA / FDA reference values](../NutritionLabelDailyValues.md)).

## Health Benefits  

Key health benefits supported by research:

- **Gut health:** Contains over 30 probiotic strains (more than yogurt), which
  help balance gut [microbiota](../../Body/Microbiome.md), reduce intestinal
  inflammation, and improve digestion.
- **Bone health:** Provides calcium, vitamin K2, magnesium, and phosphorus;
  studies show improved bone mineral density with regular consumption.
- **Blood sugar control:** May lower fasting glucose and improve insulin
  sensitivity, particularly in type 2 diabetes.
- **Cardiovascular support:** Associated with reduced LDL cholesterol, lower
  blood pressure, and improved lipid profiles.
- **Immune function:** Probiotics stimulate immune tissues in the gut; 
  B vitamins and zinc support immune resilience.
- **Lactose tolerance:** Fermentation breaks down most lactose, making it
  easier to digest for those with mild dairy sensitivities.

Kefir also contains the unique polysaccharide **kefiran**, which has
antimicrobial and antioxidant properties not found in other fermented dairy
products.

## Gut Microbiome Modulation

A 2026 systematic review of human interventional studies found kefir
consistently:

- **Increased** beneficial taxa: _Lactobacillus_, _Bifidobacterium_,
  _[Akkermansia muciniphila](../../Supplements/Akkermansia.md)_, and
  _Faecalibacterium prausnitzii_
- **Decreased** pro-inflammatory genera _Enterobacteriaceae_
- Improved stool frequency and consistency, reduced constipation
- In one study, professional athletes consuming kefir showed elevated
  _Akkermansia_ and _Faecalibacterium_, taxa linked to improved energy
  metabolism and anti-inflammatory effects

Kefir also promotes
[short-chain fatty acid (SCFA)](../../Body/ShortChainFattyAcid.md) 
production of acetate and [butyrate](../../Body/Butyrate.md) which lower 
[luminal](../../Body/GutLumen.md) pH, create a protective niche for beneficial 
bacteria, and feed colonocytes.

## Probiotic Diversity & Key Strains

Kefir contains **30 to 61 strains** of bacteria and yeasts (vs. 2 to 5 in 
yogurt), making it one of the most microbially diverse fermented foods. The 
standout is **_Lactobacillus kefiri_**, found **only** in kefir.  

Other notable strains include:
* _L. plantarum_ (reduces oxidative stress markers like MDA)
* _L. kefiranofaciens_ (reduces intestinal bleeding from EHEC infection)
* _Kluyveromyces marxianus_ (a yeast with strong adhesion to intestinal cells 
  and resistance to gastric/bile conditions).

## Kefiran: The Unique Polysaccharide

Kefiran is a water-soluble glucogalactan (galactose + glucose + acetic acid)
that makes up **over half the dry mass** of kefir grains. It is the compound
that distinguishes kefir from every other fermented dairy. 

- Inhibits growth of _Salmonella_, _H. pylori_, and _E. coli_
- Significantly lowered triglycerides, total cholesterol, and LDL while raising
  HDL in animal models
- Reduced leptin mRNA expression in adipose tissue, suggesting anti-obesity
  potential
- Survives gastric acid and bile, adheres to intestinal epithelium, and produces
  antibacterial compounds

Its documented activities:

| **Property**      | **Mechanism**                                                                                                                                                                                             |
|:------------------|:----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Prebiotic         | Resists GI enzymatic hydrolysis; ferments in the colon, stimulating _Bifidobacterium_ and _Lactobacillus_ proliferation (2.1-fold increase in vitro)                                                      |
| Immunomodulatory  | Increases secretory [IgA](../../Body/ImmuneSystem/ImmunoglobulinA.md) in intestinal mucosa; enhances peritoneal macrophage activity                                                                       |
| Anti-inflammatory | Reduces TNF-α, IL-8, and IL-6; inhibits NF-κB signaling                                                                                                                                                   |
| Antimicrobial     | Protects epithelial cells against _Bacillus cereus_ exocellular factors; antifungal activity against _Candida_                                                                                            |
| Antitumor         | Water-soluble fraction retards tumor growth and pulmonary metastasis in mice; water-insoluble fraction inhibits melanoma metastasis                                                                       |
| Cardiovascular    | Reduces hypertension-induced blood pressure; ACE-inhibitory activity                                                                                                                                      |
| Hepatic           | A 2026 study showed kefiran (K1A) mitigated high-fat-diet-induced fatty liver disease (MASLD) by restoring gut dysbiosis and enhancing one-carbon folate metabolism — a novel gut-liver axis intervention |

## Cardiovascular & Metabolic Effects

- **Cholesterol:** Multiple strains (_L. kefiri_, _L. acidophilus_ LA15, _L.
  plantarum_ B23) lowered serum total cholesterol, LDL, and triglycerides in
  rats fed a high-cholesterol diet. _L. kefiri_ DH5 reduced body weight, adipose
  tissue, and plasma lipids by upregulating PPARα in adipose tissue.
- **Blood pressure:** Kefiran has ACE-inhibitory properties, suggesting a
  mechanism for blood pressure reduction independent of probiotic action.
- **Blood sugar:** Kefir significantly reduced HbA1C in clinical trials. _L.
  rhamnosus_ Lb102 and _Bifidobacterium_ Bf141 improved glucose tolerance and
  [insulin sensitivity](../../Body/InsulinResistance.md), reduced [visceral fat](../../Body/VisceralFat.md) 
  and inflammation in metabolic syndrome models.
- **Obesity:** The _L. kefiri_ DH5 strain decreased body weight and adipose
  tissue in high-fat-diet mice through reduced intestinal cholesterol and PPARα
  upregulation.

## Immune System Support

- Probiotics in kefir stimulate **gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT)**,
  increasing production of anti-inflammatory cytokines and immunoglobulins
- Kefiran specifically boosts **secretory IgA**, the first-line mucosal defense
  against enteric pathogens
- _L. kefiri_ enhances mucosal immune response and reduces gut inflammation
- B vitamins (B12, riboflavin) and zinc in kefir support overall immune
  resilience

## Bone & Joint Health

Beyond calcium and vitamin K2, kefir peptides and kefiran:

- Reduce pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-17)
- Inhibit NF-κB signaling
- Modulate the **RANKL–OPG balance**, a key osteoimmune pathway
- Animal and pilot human studies show improved bone mineral density with regular
  consumption

## Kefir vs. Yogurt

| **Feature**           | **Kefir**                              | **Yogurt (plain)**  | **Greek Yogurt**  |
|:----------------------|:---------------------------------------|:--------------------|:------------------|
| **Probiotic strains** | 30 to 61                               | 2 to 5              | 2 to 5            |
| **Protein/cup**       | 8 to 11 g                              | 8 to 9 g            | 15 to 20 g        |
| **Calcium/cup**       | 300 t0 400 mg                          | 300 to 400 mg       | 250 to 350 mg     |
| **B vitamins**        | Higher (longer fermentation and yeast) | Lower               | Lower             |
| **Vitamin A & D**     | Higher                                 | Lower               | Lower             |
| **Lactose**           | ~30% less than milk                    | ~20% less than milk | Similar to yogurt |
| **Texture**           | Drinkable, slightly effervescent       | Spoonable           | Thick, spoonable  |

Kefir leads on **microbial diversity, B vitamins, vitamin A/D, and
kefiran-specific benefits**. Greek yogurt leads on **protein concentration**.
For gut health and probiotic diversity, kefir is the stronger choice.

## Practical Considerations

- **Dosage:** Most studies use **1 cup (240 mL) per day** as the effective dose
- **Lactose:** Fermentation reduces lactose by ~30%, but kefir is **not**
  lactose-free. Those with severe intolerance may still react
- **[Alcohol](../../Avoid/Alcohol.md):** Traditional kefir contains trace alcohol
  (0.1–1.5%) from fermentation
- **Store-bought vs. homemade:** Store-bought kefir is often pasteurized
  (killing live cultures). Homemade or "live culture" brands retain the full
  probiotic benefit.
- **Caveat:** A 2026 review noted that some commercial "kefir" products are
  mislabeled or fortified with additional probiotic strains, making it unclear
  whether observed effects come from the kefir matrix or the added strains.

## Homemade Milk Kefir

To ferment milk kefir at home, combine **milk kefir grains** with fresh milk and
let it sit at room temperature for **12 to 24 hours** until it thickens and
turns tangy.

#### Tips for Choosing

- **Live (fresh) grains** are preferred over dehydrated — they're more active
  and produce kefir faster.
- Look for sellers who ship quickly (First Class or Priority) so grains don't
  sit in transit.
- Ask for **whole milk** (not UHT/ultra-pasteurized) to activate the grains —
  UHT milk can prevent proper fermentation.
- A 1 tsp to 1 tbsp starter amount is enough for your first batch (~1 quart of
  milk).

### Instructions  

1. **Combine:** Add about 1 tablespoon of kefir grains to 1 cup of fresh whole
   milk in a clean glass jar. Leave about an inch of headspace.
2. **Cover:** Top with a coffee filter, paper towel, or cloth secured with a
   rubber band. Avoid an airtight lid — the ferment produces CO₂.
3. **Ferment:** Leave in a warm, dark spot (68–76°F / 20–24°C) for **12 to 24
   hours**. The milk is done when it thickens to a cream-like consistency and
   develops a sour, tangy smell. It may separate into curds and whey — that's
   normal.
4. **Strain:** Pour the kefir through a fine-mesh (non-metal) sieve into a bowl
   or bottle. Gently push the liquid through with a plastic or wooden spoon. The
   grains remain in the strainer.
5. **Repeat:** Return the grains to a clean jar, add fresh milk, and start the
   next batch. No need to rinse the grains.

### Tips

- **Milk choice:** Whole cow's milk works best. Avoid ultra-pasteurized or
  lactose-free milk.
- **Temperature matters:** Warmer rooms speed up fermentation; cooler rooms slow
  it down.
- **Second fermentation (optional):** After straining, bottle the kefir with a
  tight cap and leave at room temperature another 12 to 24 hours for extra 
  fizz and tang. Add fruit, ginger, or lemon peel for flavor.
- **Storage:** Finished kefir keeps in the fridge for up to 2 weeks.
- **Grain growth:** Grains roughly double every 2 weeks. Share extras, freeze a
  backup batch, or add more milk to keep the ratio balanced.
- **Light:** Indirect or ambient light is perfectly fine. The main concern is
  **direct sunlight**, which can heat the jar unevenly, stress the culture, and
  encourage unwanted microbial growth. The standard recommendation is to keep 
  the jar out of direct sun in a cupboard, pantry, or shaded counter. Total 
  darkness is not required. 
  A few specific effects of light:
  - **Riboflavin (vitamin B₂)** is degraded by UV and visible light, so 
    prolonged exposure can reduce this nutrient.
  - **Heat is the real risk** — sunlight through a window can raise the
    temperature enough to speed fermentation, upset the bacteria/yeast 
    balance, or cause over-fermentation.
  - **Indirect light** (a kitchen counter or bright room) has no meaningful 
    negative effect on the fermentation process itself.

### Resting Milk Kefir Grains

| **Break Length**    | **Method**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
|:--------------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Up to 2 weeks       | Put grains in a jar with enough fresh milk to cover them generously (at least 2 cups per tablespoon of grains), seal with a lid, and refrigerate.                                                                                               |
| 2 to 4 weeks        | Same as above, but change the milk once mid-trip if possible.                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Longer than a month | Rinse grains, coat them in powdered milk, seal in a container, and **freeze** (up to ~1 year). Alternatively, air-dry on parchment for 3–5 days, then store in an airtight bag with a spoonful of powdered milk in the fridge (up to 6 months). |

#### Tips

- Keep the jar in the **back of the fridge** (not the door) for a steadier
  temperature.
- Leave the lid slightly ajar to allow gas to escape.
- On return, **strain and discard** the storage liquid, then feed the grains
  fresh milk. Expect 1 or 2 "wake-up" batches before quality returns to normal.
- For any break over 3 weeks, it's smart to keep a small **backup portion**
  frozen or dried separately.

### Backup Starter

When your grains have multiplied enough set aside a small portion. About 
**1 tablespoon** is plenty. Mmost people end up with extra after a few weeks of 
regular fermentation. Preserve it using one of these methods:

#### Freezing (easiest, up to ~1 year)

1. **Rinse** the spare grains in clean, non-chlorinated water or fresh milk.
2. **Pat dry** on a clean paper towel, then let them air-dry for **15 to 30 
   minutes** (up to 24 hours for best results).
3. Place them in a **zip lock freezer bag or small jar**.
4. Coat them with a spoonful of **powdered milk** (acts as a cryoprotectant 
   against freezer burn).  
5. **Seal** the bag/jar, **label** with the date, and store it in the freezer.

> A popular shortcut many fermenters use: skip the rinsing entirely and just
> scoop the extra grains straight into a small bag, cover with a couple inches of
> fresh whole milk, and freeze. It works well.

#### Dehydrating (6+ months)

1. Rinse and gently blot the grains dry.
2. Spread them in a **thin, even layer** on parchment paper or a paper towel.
3. Let them air-dry at room temperature (out of direct sun, away from heat) for
   **3 to 5 days**, turning occasionally, until they're **hard and brittle**.
4. Store in a **sealed jar or zip lock bag** with a pinch of powdered milk in 
   the **fridge or freezer**.

#### Reviving your backup

- **Frozen**: Thaw in the fridge for ~24 hours, strain off the liquid, then feed
  fresh milk/sugar water daily until the kefir thickens in 24 hours (usually
  3–14 days).
- **Dried**: Rehydrate in a little fresh water for a couple of hours first, then
  feed daily as above.

The key is to **label everything** (date, grain type, method) so you don't
forget what you have or when you put it away.
