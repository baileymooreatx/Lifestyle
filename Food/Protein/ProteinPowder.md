# Plant-based Protein Powder

Grind these in small batches until fine powder, then mix:

| **Ingredient**          | **Amount** |
|:------------------------|:-----------|
| Chia seeds              | ½ cup      |
| Hemp hearts             | ½ cup      |
| Pumpkin seeds (toasted) | ½ cup      |
| Flax seeds              | ¼ cup      |
| Sunflower seeds         | ¼ cup      |

**Key tips:**

- Use a **spice/coffee grinder** for the finest texture; a blender works but
  use 10 to 15 sec pulses to avoid turning seeds into butter.
- Keep the grinder bowl **dry** — moisture makes chia gel into paste.
- Store in an **airtight container in the fridge** up to 1 month (or freezer for
  up to 6 months).
- Use ~4 tbsp (one scoop) per serving; it yields roughly **15 to 21 g of
  protein** per serving depending on the blend.

## Toast Pumpkin Seeds

You can use raw pumpkin seeds directly, the protein content is essentially
identical (~30 g per 100 g) whether raw or roasted. However, a **light toast is
recommended** for three practical reasons.

- **Flavor** – raw seeds taste slightly bitter/grassy; toasting brings out a
  nuttier profile in the powder.
- **Grindability** – toasting removes a bit of moisture, making seeds more
  brittle and less likely to turn into a paste.
- **Oil release** – dry seeds are less likely to release oils during grinding
  (the main cause of "seed butter" instead of powder).

**Quick toast method:** spread raw pepitas (shelled, unsalted) on a baking sheet
and bake at **300 °F for 5–8 minutes**, then cool completely before grinding.

Raw seeds have slightly higher **phytic acid**, which can modestly reduce 
absorption of minerals like zinc and magnesium. Roasting reduces phytic acid, so 
the lightly toasted version gives you a small edge on mineral bioavailability. 
For protein specifically, it makes no difference.
