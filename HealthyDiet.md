# Healthy Diet

## Manage macronutrients to lower insulin and reduce inflammation  

1. **Control carbohydrates**
   1. No [ultra processed foods](./Avoid/Ultra-processedFood.md)  
   2. Minimize [sugar](./Avoid/Sugar.md)  
   3. Avoid boxes and barcodes by eating lots of plants.  
      See [Eat 30 plants per week](Food/Eat30Plants.md).  
      *  **Vegetables**  
         * Leafy greens: arugula, [spinach](Food/Vegetables/Spinach.md), kale, 
           chard, collards, lettuce    
         * [Cruciferous vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruciferous_vegetables)  
         * [Beets](./Supplements/BeetrootPowder.md), carrots, sweet potatoes, yams  
         * [Peppers](Food/Vegetables/ChiliPepper.md), cucumber, eggplant, 
           [onions](Food/Vegetables/Onion.md), leeks, and scallions  
      *  **Fruits**  
         * Berries: [blueberries](Food/Fruits/Blueberry.md), [strawberries](Food/Fruits/Strawberry.md),
           blackberries, raspberries  
         * Pomegranate (seeds or fresh, unsweetened)  
         * [Tart cherries](Food/Fruits/TartCherry.md)  
         * Citrus: [oranges](Food/Fruits/Orange.md), [lemons](Food/Fruits/Lemon.md),
           limes, and grapefruit  
           **Whole fruit, not juice**  
         * [Apples](Food/Fruits/Apple.md) in moderation, with the skin  
         * Grapes in moderation, dark or red preferred for resveratrol  
2. **Prioritize [protein](Food/Protein/Protein.md)**  
   Eat one gram of protein per pound for your ideal weight.
   * Wild-caught, low-mercury fish: [salmon](Food/Protein/Salmon.md), sardines, 
     mackerel, anchovies
   * [Eggs](Food/Protein/Egg.md): Pasture raised or organic when possible  
   * Grass fed beef, bison, venison, lamb
   * Chicken and turkey (check labels for additives and chlorine)  
     Pasture raised when possible
3. Fat is good, but avoid [seed oils](./Food/Fats/SeedOil.md) because they are 
   too high in Omega-6 fatty acids. _This advice may be flawed._  
   **Prefer healthy oils**
   1. [Extra virgin olive oil](Food/Fats/ExtraVirginOliveOil.md) (EVOO) first cold pressed  
      Ideally organic, not blended, and stored in dark glass to prevent oxidation  
      Olive oil is frequently adulterated. Look for authenticity. Peppery finish.  
   2. [Avocados](Food/Vegetables/Avocado.md) and Avocado oil (cold pressed and tested)   
   3. [Coconut oil](Food/Fats/CoconutOil.md) is good for cooking at high temperature.  
   4. Grass fed butter or ghee, beef tallow  
   5. [Nuts](Food/Nuts/Nuts.md): 
      * [Walnuts](Food/Nuts/Walnut.md)
      * [Almonds](Food/Nuts/Almond.md) 
      * [Brazil nuts](Food/Nuts/BrazilNut.md)    
        Limit Brazil nuts because they are very high in Selenium
   6. Seeds
      * [Flaxseed](Food/Seeds/Flaxseed.md)  
      * [Chia](Food/Seeds/Chia.md)  
      * [Hemp](Food/Seeds/HempHearts.md) 
      * [Pumpkin](Food/Seeds/Pumpkin.md) 
      * [Sunflower](Food/Seeds/Sunflower.md) (raw preferred)
4. Prefer **fewer ingredients**  
   When you cook it yourself, you control the ingredients.  
5. **Stop Snacking**  
   Avoid grazing throughout the day to keep insulin levels low, aiming for three
   distinct meals daily.
6. Eat in the right order to prevent insulin spikes.
   Drink apple cider vinegar and water before 20 minutes before each meal. 
   Consider adding [inulin](./Supplements/Inulin.md) to increase fiber.   
   1. [Fiber Firewall](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JdZBQWRBSpg&list=WL&index=11&t=799s)  
      Eat salad or vegetables first.
   2. [Protein and Fat Anchor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JdZBQWRBSpg&list=WL&index=11&t=1032s)  
      Protein releases [GLP-1](./Body/SatietyHormones.md) to slow digestion and
      decrease insulin release.  
      Fat increases satiety.
   3. [Carbohydrates last](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JdZBQWRBSpg&list=WL&index=11&t=1278s)  
      Blood sugar increases slowly when carbs come last, avoiding a flood of 
      insulin to lower glucose.
7. **[Walk](./Walk.md) After Meals**  
   Use skeletal muscle as a "metabolic engine" by lifting weights 2 or 3 times a
   week (with adequate protein intake). A 10-minute walk immediately after meals
   helps lower blood sugar by raising GLUT-4, so muscles can absorb glucose
   without insulin.
8. **Master Meal Timing**  
   Stop eating at least three hours before bedtime to allow for optimal
   nocturnal fat burning and recovery.
9. **24-Hour Water Fast**  
   Once a week, perform a 24-hour water-only fast to trigger autophagy (cell
   cleanup) and increase human growth hormone levels.
10. **Achieve Metabolic Flexibility**  
    By mastering the previous habits, the body becomes capable of efficiently 
    switching between burning sugar and fat.

### Additional Recommendations

[Ben Azadi](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_DXmxRAlDuY) advocates for specific 
supplementation, specifically [creatine](./Supplements/Creatine.md) and 
[magnesium glycinate](./Supplements/MagnesiumGlycinate.md), to support muscle 
recovery and sleep, which are vital for metabolic repair.

## Diet Comparison

|                            | **Keto**                         | **Atkins**                         | **Mediterranean**                 | **Green Mediterranean**                   |
|:---------------------------|:---------------------------------|:-----------------------------------|:----------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Carbs**                  | 5–10% of calories (~20–50 g/day) | Low to very low (phase-dependent)  | 40–50% of calories                | 40–50% of calories                        |
| **Protein**                | 20–25%                           | Moderate to high                   | 15–20%                            | 15–20%                                    |
| **Fat**                    | 65–80%                           | Variable (higher in early phases)  | 30–40%                            | 30–40%                                    |
| **Key mechanism**          | Induces ketosis (fat-burning)    | Carb restriction + protein satiety | Fiber, volume, nutrient density   | Same + walnuts, green tea, Mankai algae\* |
| **Meat**                   | Allowed (often high)             | Allowed in moderation              | Limited red meat                  | Eliminated (no red/processed meat)        |
| **Fruits & grains**        | Eliminated or severely limited   | Restricted in early phases         | Core daily staples                | Core daily staples + more leafy greens    |
| **Weight-loss speed**      | Fast (water + fat loss)          | Fast initially                     | Gradual (~20 lbs/year)            | Gradual                                   |
| **Sustainability**         | Low                              | Low                                | High                              | High                                      |
| **Strongest evidence for** | Short-term weight loss, epilepsy | Short-term weight loss             | Heart health, longevity, diabetes | Cardiometabolic benefits (amplified)      |

### Key Contrasts

- **Keto vs. Atkins**: Both are low-carb, but keto is stricter (≤50 g net carbs,
  70%+ fat) and targets sustained ketosis. Atkins is phase-based and less rigid
  about fat percentage.
- **Mediterranean vs. Green Mediterranean**: The Green version adds walnuts,
  green tea, and Mankai algae (see below) while eliminating red and processed
  meats and boosting leafy greens. Research suggests this amplifies the
  cardiometabolic benefits of standard Mediterranean eating.
- **Restrictive vs. permissive**: Keto and Atkins restrict entire food groups
  (grains, most fruits, legumes); both Mediterranean variants are permissive,
  emphasizing *what to eat* over *what to avoid*.

### How They Compare to Federal Dietary Guidelines

The **Dietary Guidelines for Americans** (published by USDA and HHS) 2020–2025
recommend:

| **Macro**     | **DGA range** | **Keto** | **Atkins**      | **Mediterranean** | **Green Mediterranean** |
|:--------------|--------------:|---------:|:----------------|------------------:|------------------------:|
| Carbohydrates |        45–65% |  ❌ 5–10% | ❌ very low      |          ✅ 40–50% |                ✅ 40–50% |
| Protein       |        10–35% | ✅ 20–25% | ✅ moderate-high |          ✅ 15–20% |                ✅ 15–20% |
| Fat           |        20–35% | ❌ 65–80% | ⚠️ variable     |          ✅ 30–40% |                ✅ 30–40% |

The Mediterranean and Green Mediterranean diets fall squarely within DGA ranges.
Keto and Atkins both fall well outside the recommended fat and carbohydrate
ranges. The DGA also includes a "Healthy Mediterranean-Style Eating Pattern" as
one of its recommended models, giving it the strongest alignment of the four.

> **Note**: The DGA is a general population guideline, not a prescription.
> Individual needs (e.g., diabetes management, epilepsy) may justify
> deviations. Consult a physician or registered dietitian before starting a 
> restrictive diet.  

### Mankai Duckweed

Despite the name "mankai algae" sometimes used colloquially, it's **not an
algae**. It's the world's smallest flowering plant, *Wolffia globosa*, a type of 
[duckweed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemnoideae) that floats on still 
freshwater. In the Green Mediterranean diet, it's consumed as a 100 g daily 
frozen cube blended into a green shake.  

#### Why It's Notable

| **Nutrient** | **Per ~85 g serving**                                 |
|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| Protein      | ~5 g complete protein (all 9 essential amino acids)   |
| Iron         | 45% DV (bioavailable; reversed anemia in rat trials)  |
| Folate       | 60% DV                                                |
| Vitamin B12  | ~15% DV (bioactive cobalamin, no pseudo-B12 detected) |
| Vitamin K    | 580% DV                                               |
| Vitamin A    | 30% DV                                                |
| Fiber        | ~4 g                                                  |
| Calories     | ~45 kcal                                              |

#### Key Distinctions

- **Complete protein** with an amino acid profile similar to egg — rare among
  plant foods.
- **Vitamin B12 in a plant** is unusual; most plant "B12" sources contain
  inactive analogs. Mankai's B12 has been shown to be bioactive and
  well-absorbed in humans.
- **~200 distinct polyphenols** (quercetin, catechins, kaempferol, etc.),
  contributing to the antioxidant effects seen in the Green-MED trials.
- **Low glycemic impact** — a crossover trial showed a Mankai shake produced a
  lower post-meal glucose peak than a matched yogurt shake.

#### Role in the Green-MED Evidence

In the 18-month DIRECT-PLUS trial (~300 adults), the Mankai-containing group
showed:

- Greater reductions in blood proteins linked to **brain aging** (especially
  Galectin-9)
- ~50% less hippocampal shrinkage vs. standard Mediterranean
- **39% reduction in liver fat** (vs. 20% for standard Med)
- Improved insulin sensitivity and lipid profiles

The researchers emphasize it's the **combination** of Mankai, green tea, and 
walnuts that drives the amplified benefits, not any single ingredient in
isolation.
