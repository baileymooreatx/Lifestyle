# Parsley

Parsley is remarkably nutrient-dense for such a low-calorie herb — just **22
calories per cup** (60 g) of fresh chopped parsley. Here's what makes it stand
out:

## Key Vitamins

| Nutrient      | Per 1 cup (60 g)  | % Daily Value |
|---------------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Vitamin K** | 984 µg            | **820%**      |
| **Vitamin C** | 80 mg             | **89%**       |
| **Vitamin A** | 253 µg (5,054 IU) | **28%**       |
| Folate (B9)   | 91 µg             | 23%           |
| Iron          | 4 mg              | 47%           |
| Calcium       | 83 mg             | 8%            |
| Potassium     | 332 mg            | 7%            |

## Main Health Benefits

- **Bone health** — The extremely high vitamin K content (a single tablespoon
  covers >70% of your daily need) is critical for bone mineralization and proper
  calcium metabolism.
- **Immune support** — Nearly 90% of your daily vitamin C in a single cup, plus
  antioxidants like **apigenin** and **luteolin** (flavonoids) that help reduce
  inflammation.
- **Eye health** — Rich in **beta-carotene**, **lutein**, and **zeaxanthin**,
  carotenoids that protect against age-related vision decline.
- **Antioxidant protection** — Parsley's flavonoids and carotenoids help combat
  oxidative stress, which is linked to chronic diseases like heart disease and
  certain cancers.
- **Kidney & digestive support** — Parsley has natural **diuretic** properties
  that may help flush excess sodium and support kidney function. Its fiber (~2
  g/cup) also aids regularity.
- **Blood sugar regulation** — Some research suggests compounds in parsley may
  help improve insulin sensitivity.

## Worth Noting

- It's also a good source of **magnesium**, **copper**, and **zinc** in smaller
  amounts.
- Because it's so nutrient-concentrated, even a modest garnish of 1–2
  tablespoons delivers meaningful vitamins.
- The main caution: very large amounts (especially parsley *tea* or concentrated
  extracts) can be problematic during **pregnancy** due to its emmenagogue
  properties, and the high vitamin K can interact with **blood-thinning
  medications** like warfarin.

In short, parsley is one of the most nutrient-dense foods by weight — a
genuinely healthy habit to sprinkle generously over your food.

## Washing

Wash right before use, not in advance. Do not scrub, blast with strong water, 
or use soap or produce wash on these tender herbs.

1. **Fill a large bowl** with cold water (enough to fully submerge the herbs).
   Add a splash of white vinegar (about ¼ cup per ~10 cups of water) if you want
   to help lift grit or residue, though plain cold water is usually enough.
2. **Remove any ties and wilted leaves**, drop the parsley in, and **gently 
   swish** with your hand for 10 to 20 seconds to loosen dirt.
3. **Let the grit settle** to the bottom, then **lift the herbs out** rather
   than draining the water back over them (this keeps sand from re-clinging).
   Refill and repeat if the water looks dirty.
4. **Dry well** — a **salad spinner** is the fastest, or pat/roll them in
   paper towels. Thorough drying is the key to keeping them from going slimy.  

## Storage

The best method depends on how long you need it to last:

**Jar method (best for 1 to 4 weeks):**

1. Trim ~½ inch off the stem ends.
2. Place the bunch upright in a jar or glass with about **1 inch of water** so 
   the stems are submerged and the leaves are dry.
3. Loosely cover the leaves with a **plastic bag** (the grocery produce bag
   works fine).
4. Refrigerate. **Change the water every 2 to 3 days** and remove any yellowing
   leaves.

This is the method most home cooks and test kitchens found to be the clear winner.

**Paper towel + bag (best for 3 to 10 days):**

1. Wash and **dry thoroughly**.
2. Wrap the bunch loosely in a **damp paper towel**.
3. Place in a **resealable plastic bag**, press out excess air, and refrigerate.
4. Re-dampen or swap the towel every ~2 days.

**Freezing (best for months):**

- Chop parsley and freeze in **ice cube trays with olive oil** (great for
  cooking), or press into flat "cigars" in a zip-top bag.
- Lasts **6+ months**. Not ideal for garnish, but perfect for soups, sauces, and
  cooked dishes.

### Key tips

- **Don't wash before storing** if using the jar method — dry stems in water
  last longest.
- **Dry thoroughly** before any bag/towel method; trapped moisture is the main
  cause of slimy, spoiled parsley.
- Store away from strong-smelling produce (onions, garlic) to prevent flavor
  transfer.
