# Autoimmunity Disorders

**Autoimmune disorders** (also called autoimmune diseases) are a group of
chronic conditions in which the body's **adaptive immune system** mistakenly
identifies its own healthy cells and tissues as foreign invaders and attacks
them. Normally, the immune system distinguishes "self" from "non-self," but in
autoimmunity this self-tolerance breaks down, leading to the production of 
**autoantibodies** and **autoreactive T cells** that cause inflammation and
tissue damage.

Key points:

- **Prevalence:** More than 80 recognized autoimmune diseases exist (some
  estimates exceed 100), affecting roughly 5–10% of the population.
- **Classification:** They are broadly divided into **organ-specific**
  (targeting one organ, e.g., Hashimoto's disease targeting the thyroid) and 
  **systemic** (affecting multiple organs, e.g., systemic lupus erythematosus).
- **Causes:** The exact trigger is unknown, but a combination of **genetic
  predisposition, environmental factors** (infections, smoking, diet), and
  **hormonal influences** is believed to play a role.
- **Symptoms:** Vary widely depending on the organ affected but commonly include
  fatigue, joint pain/swelling, fever, skin rashes, and digestive issues.
- **Treatment:** There is no cure, but many autoimmune diseases can be managed
  with immunosuppressive medications, anti-inflammatory drugs, and other
  therapies aimed at calming the overactive immune response.

## Examples

| Disease                            | Cause                                                                      | Description                                                                                                         |
|------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Rheumatoid arthritis               | Autoantibodies (anti-CCP, rheumatoid factor) and immune complex deposition | Chronic inflammation of joints and surrounding tissues, leading to pain, swelling, and deformity                    |
| Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) | Autoantibodies (ANA, anti-dsDNA, anti-Smith)                               | Systemic disorder affecting skin, joints, kidneys, brain, and other organs                                          |
| Celiac disease                     | Immune reaction to gluten (wheat, rye, barley)                             | Damage to the lining of the small intestine, causing malabsorption and digestive symptoms                           |
| Psoriasis                          | Various autoantibodies (not specific)                                      | Autoimmune skin condition causing redness, irritation, and thick, flaky, silver-white patches                       |
| Hashimoto's disease                | Autoantibodies (anti-TPO, anti-Tg) attacking the thyroid                   | Inflammation of the thyroid gland leading to hypothyroidism                                                         |
| Addison's disease                  | Autoimmune destruction of the adrenal cortex                               | Adrenal hormone insufficiency causing fatigue, weight loss, and low blood pressure                                  |
| Graves' disease                    | Stimulating (agonist) anti-thyroid antibodies                              | Overactive thyroid gland triggered by autoantibodies, causing hyperthyroidism                                       |
| Type 1 diabetes                    | Immune system attacks insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas         | The body cannot produce insulin, leading to high blood sugar                                                        |
| Multiple sclerosis (MS)            | Unknown; oligoclonal bands often present in CSF                            | Autoimmune demyelination of the central nervous system, causing neurological deficits                               |
| Myasthenia gravis                  | Anti-acetylcholine receptor (anti-AChR) or anti-MuSK antibodies            | Autoimmune attack on the neuromuscular junction causing muscle weakness and fatigue                                 |
| Crohn's disease                    | Immune-mediated inflammation of the intestinal tract                       | A form of inflammatory bowel disease characterized by chronic intestinal inflammation, abdominal pain, and diarrhea |
| Sjögren's syndrome                 | Anti-SSA/Ro, anti-SSB/La antibodies                                        | Autoimmune attack on exocrine glands (salivary and lacrimal), causing dry eyes and mouth                            |
| Scleroderma (systemic sclerosis)   | ANA, anti-Scl-70, anti-centromere antibodies                               | Autoimmune thickening and hardening of the skin and connective tissues, potentially affecting internal organs       |
| Guillain-Barré syndrome            | Various antibodies (anti-GM1, anti-GD1a)                                   | Acute immune-mediated inflammatory disorder of the peripheral nervous system causing progressive weakness           |
| Pernicious anemia                  | Autoantibodies against intrinsic factor or parietal cells                  | Inability to absorb vitamin B12, leading to megaloblastic anemia and neurological symptoms                          |
