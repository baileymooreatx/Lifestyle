# Commensal Bacteria

Commensal bacteria are the trillions of resident microbes that colonize the
human body — primarily the gut, but also the skin, oral cavity, and respiratory
tract — in a relationship that is formally defined as **commensalism** (the
microbe benefits; the host is neither helped nor harmed). In practice, the
relationship is closer to **mutualism**: both parties gain. The term "commensal"
persists as a historical convention, but modern immunology treats the gut
microbiota as a functional organ that the host **requires**.  

Commensal bacteria are not passive passengers. They are **active architects** of
immune architecture — training [T cells](./T-Cell.md), tuning 
[pattern recognition receptor (PRR)](./PatternRecognitionReceptor.md) 
sensitivity, producing metabolites that regulate distant organs, and maintaining 
the ecological barrier that prevents pathogen invasion. A germ-free immune 
system is fundamentally broken.  

## Taxonomic Composition

The gut harbors ~300–1,000 species across a few dominant phyla:

| **Phylum**                          | **Key Genera**                                                                        | **Share** |
|-------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------|
| **Bacteroidota** (Bacteroidetes)    | *Bacteroides*, *Prevotella*                                                           | ~30–50%   |
| **Bacillota** (Firmicutes)          | *Clostridium*, *Faecalibacterium*, *Ruminococcus*, *Lactobacillus*, *Bifidobacterium* | ~30–50%   |
| **Actinomycetota**                  | *Bifidobacterium*, *Mycobacterium*                                                    | ~1–5%     |
| **Pseudomonadota** (Proteobacteria) | *Escherichia*, *Klebsiella*                                                           | ~1%       |
| **Verrucomicrobiota**               | *Akkermansia muciniphila*                                                             | < 1%      |

The first two phyla together account for **~90%** of the gut microbiota.

## How They Interact with the Immune System

Commensal bacteria are the *primary ligand source* for 
[Troll-like Receptors (TLRs)](./Troll-LikeReceptor.md) and 
[NOD-like Receptors (NLRs)](./NOD-LikeReceptor.md) in steady state. By 
interacting with these 
[pattern recognition receptors (PRRs)](./PatternRecognitionReceptor.md) they 
provide essential functions.

### Innate Immune "Toning"

- Commensal peptidoglycan continuously stimulates **NOD1/NOD2** in intestinal
  epithelial cells and **TLR2/TLR4** at the mucosal surface
- This tonic signaling keeps defense genes in an **open chromatin state** (
  epigenetically primed), enabling a rapid response when a true pathogen arrives
- Germ-free mice lack this priming and are more susceptible to infection

### Adaptive Immune Education

- Commensal antigens drive **CD4⁺ Treg** differentiation (via IL-10, TGF-β, and
  [SCFA](./ShortChainFattyAcid.md)-dependent pathways) — the immune system's 
  "tolerance" arm.
- They also promote **Th17** cells (via IL-22) that maintain barrier integrity
  and antimicrobial peptide production
- **B cells** in Peyer's patches undergo class-switching to produce secretory
  [IgA](./ImmunoglobulinA.md) (sIgA), which coats commensals and prevents their
  translocation into the lamina propria — a form of immune "compartmentalization"

### Metabolic Contributions

- **[SCFAs](./ShortChainFattyAcid.md)** (butyrate, propionate, acetate) from 
  fiber fermentation → Treg induction, epithelial barrier reinforcement, 
  anti-inflammatory signaling
- **Bile acid metabolism** (e.g., 7α-dehydroxylation by *Clostridium*
  clusters) → FXR receptor activation → mucosal barrier maintenance
- **Vitamin synthesis** (K, B₁₂, folate)
- **Short-chain fatty acids** also serve as the primary energy source for
  colonocytes

### Colonization Resistance

Commensals occupy available **niches** (adhesion sites, nutrient pools) and
produce **bacteriocins**, reactive oxygen species, and modified pH that
inhibit pathogen establishment. This is called **competitive exclusion**. 
The immune system's first line of defense is often *not* immune at all, but 
ecological.

### Paneth Cell Symbiosis

- A striking example of commensal–host crosstalk: commensal bacteria signal
  through **NOD2** and **LRRK2** in Paneth cells to direct selective sorting of
  **lysozyme** into secretory vesicles, ensuring lysozyme is delivered to the
  mucus layer where it kills invading pathogens
- Loss of NOD2 or LRRK2 (both IBD-associated genes) disrupts this sorting and
  predisposes to intestinal inflammation

## The Tolerance Paradox

The immune system must simultaneously:

- **Tolerate** ~10¹⁴ commensal bacteria (avoiding chronic inflammation)
- **Remain vigilant** against pathogens

This is achieved through:

- **Physical barrier:** tight junctions, mucus layer  
- **[sIgA](./ImmunoglobulinA.md) coating:** neutralizes commensals without
  triggering inflammation  
- **Treg dominance:** at the mucosa (IL-10, TGF-β)  
- **Selective [Pattern Recognition Receptor (PRR)](./PatternRecognitionReceptor.md) 
  engagement:** commensal pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) trigger
  *low-grade* TLR/NLR signaling (homeostatic), while pathogen PAMPs at higher
  concentration or in the wrong compartment trigger *high-grade* inflammatory
  responses  

When this balance breaks it causes **IBD**, **autoimmunity**, **allergy**, or 
**infection susceptibility**.
