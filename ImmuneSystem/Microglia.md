# Microglia

**Microglia** are the **resident macrophages of the central nervous system**, 
the brain's and spinal cord's primary innate immune cells. They account for
~5–10% of all brain cells and are the *only* leukocytes that reside within the
brain parenchyma behind the blood-brain barrier (BBB).  

In health, they patrol, prune synapses, and support neurons. In disease, they 
become the primary engine of **neuroinflammation**, making them both protectors 
and destroyers of the Central Nervous System (CNS), the same "elegant defense" 
paradox that runs through the entire immune system.


## Origin

Unlike other glial cells (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes) which derive from 
**neuroectoderm**, microglia originate from **erythro-myeloid progenitors (EMPs)
in the embryonic yolk sac**. They invade the developing brain around embryonic
day 8.5 to 9.5 (mouse) or ~4.5 to 5.5 weeks gestation (human), before the 
BBB is fully formed. Once established, they **self-renew locally** throughout 
life and are *not* replenished by circulating monocytes under homeostatic 
conditions — making them a truly distinct tissue-resident population.

## Two Morphological States

| State                               | Appearance                                                                                                                           | Context                              |
|-------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Ramified (resting/surveillance)** | Small cell body with long, thin, branching processes that constantly extend and retract, surveying ~10% of the brain volume per cell | Healthy brain                        |
| **Amoeboid (activated)**            | Rounded cell body, thickened/retracted processes, increased phagocytic machinery                                                     | Infection, injury, neurodegeneration |

This transition (ramified → amoeboid) is the CNS analog of macrophage
"activation."

## Functions

**Homeostatic (surveillance & maintenance):**

- **Continuous patrolling** — processes scan the extracellular space for DAMPs,
  pathogens, or dying cells
- **Synaptic pruning** — during development and throughout life, microglia
  engulf and eliminate excess or weak synapses (complement C3/C1q-dependent),
  shaping neural circuits
- **Neuronal support** — secrete trophic factors (BDNF, GDNF), modulate
  neurotransmitter levels (uptake of glutamate, dopamine)
- **Phagocytosis of apoptotic cells** — silent clearance of dying neurons
  without triggering inflammation (efferocytosis)

**Effector (activated response):**

- **Phagocytosis** of pathogens, debris, and protein aggregates (amyloid-β,
  α-synuclein)
- **Antigen presentation** — upregulate MHC II and co-stimulatory molecules (
  CD80/86) to activate T cells that have crossed the BBB
- **Cytokine/chemokine secretion** — TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, CCL2, CXCL10
- **Complement production** — C3, C1q (opsonization, synapse elimination)
- **ROS / NO production** — microbicidal but neurotoxic in excess

## Pattern Recognition Receptor (PRR) Expression  

Microglia express the same PRR families we discussed:

| Pattern Recognition Receptor                | Ligand                            | Output                                                  |
|---------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| **TLR3**                                    | dsRNA (viral)                     | Type I IFN, pro-inflammatory cytokines                  |
| **TLR4**                                    | LPS                               | NF-κB → TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6                              |
| **TLR7/8/9**                                | ssRNA, CpG DNA                    | Antiviral + inflammatory                                |
| **NLRP3**                                   | K⁺ efflux, ROS, amyloid-β, ATP    | **Inflammasome** → caspase-1 → IL-1β, IL-18, pyroptosis |
| **C-type lectin receptors** (Dectin-1, MMR) | Fungal β-glucans, amyloid fibrils | NF-κB                                                   |
| **Scavenger receptors** (CD36, SR-A, RAGE)  | Oxidized LDL, amyloid-β, HSPs     | Phagocytosis                                            |

**NLRP3 in microglia** is particularly important. It is a major driver of
neuroinflammation in **Alzheimer's disease** (amyloid-β activates NLRP3 →
IL-1β → microglial hyperactivation → neuronal damage) and **Parkinson's
disease** (α-synuclein).

## Connection to the Microbiome

Gut-derived **short chain fatty acids (SCFAs)**, especially butyrate, reach 
the brain via:

- Circulating metabolites crossing the BBB
- Vagal nerve signaling
- Enteric nervous system → CNS

In the brain, butyrate:

- Suppresses **NLRP3 inflammasome** activation in microglia
- Promotes a **homeostatic (anti-inflammatory) microglial phenotype**
- Enhances **Treg** tone in the CNS
- Reduces microglial overactivation in models of Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and
  multiple sclerosis

Germ-free mice show **hyperactive, pro-inflammatory microglia** — the CNS analog
of the undertrained peripheral immune system. Dysbiosis (antibiotics, high-fat
diet) → reduced SCFAs → microglial priming toward a neuroinflammatory state.

## Clinical Relevance

| Condition                                                | Microglial Role                                                                                                               |
|----------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Alzheimer's disease**                                  | NLRP3 inflammasome activation by amyloid-β → chronic IL-1β → neurodegeneration; also impaired phagocytic clearance of plaques |
| **Parkinson's disease**                                  | α-synuclein → microglial activation → DA neuron loss (MPTP model)                                                             |
| **Multiple sclerosis**                                   | Microglia + infiltrating monocytes → demyelination, axonal damage                                                             |
| **HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder**               | Microglial activation by viral proteins (Tat, gp120) → chronic neuroinflammation                                              |
| **Stroke**                                               | Dual role: early pro-inflammatory (exacerbates injury) → late anti-inflammatory (tissue repair)                               |
| **Traumatic brain injury**                               | Acute activation → chronic neuroinflammation → secondary injury                                                               |
| **Neurodevelopmental disorders** (autism, schizophrenia) | Aberrant microglial synaptic pruning → altered circuit connectivity                                                           |
