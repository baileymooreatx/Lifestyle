# Insulin resistance

Insulin resistance is a condition in which cells in the muscles, fat, and liver
**stop responding normally to insulin**, the hormone the pancreas produces to
move glucose from the blood into cells for energy. As a result, glucose builds
up in the bloodstream, and the pancreas compensates by pumping out **more and
more insulin** (hyperinsulinemia). Eventually, the pancreas can't keep up, blood
sugar rises, and the condition progresses to **prediabetes** and then **type 2
diabetes**.

## How It Develops

1. Cells become less responsive to insulin's signal.
2. The pancreas overproduces insulin to maintain normal blood sugar. For a
   while, levels stay in a healthy range and there are **no symptoms**.
3. Over time (often 10 to 15 years), the pancreatic beta cells wear out and
   can no longer compensate.
4. Blood glucose rises into the prediabetic and diabetic range.

## Main Causes & Risk Factors

- **Excess body weight**, especially [visceral](./VisceralFatReduction.md)
  (belly) fat, is the leading cause.
- **Physical inactivity** — exercise improves insulin sensitivity and builds
  muscle that absorbs glucose.
- **Genetics / family history** of diabetes.
- **Age** (risk increases after 45).
- Certain **medications** (steroids, some antipsychotics, HIV drugs).
- Chronic **stress**, poor **sleep**, and a diet high in refined carbs and
  saturated fats.

## Signs & Signs Your Doctor Looks For

Insulin resistance is often **silent**. When signs appear, they include:

- **Acanthosis nigricans** — dark, velvety patches on the neck, armpits, or
  groin
- Skin tags
- Waist circumference > 40 in (men) or > 35 in (women)
- Blood pressure ≥ 130/80 mmHg
- High triglycerides or low HDL cholesterol
- Fatigue, post-meal sleepiness, or sugar cravings

## Why It Matters

Beyond diabetes, insulin resistance is a core component of **metabolic
syndrome** and is linked to **cardiovascular disease**, **nonalcoholic fatty
liver disease (NAFLD)**, **polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)**, and increased
risk of certain cancers. It affects roughly **1 in 3 American adults**, most of
whom don't know they have it.

## Reversal & Management

Lifestyle changes are the first line of defense: **regular physical activity**
(exercise opens glucose transport into muscle cells, bypassing the insulin
block), **weight loss**, and a **balanced diet** lower in refined sugars and
processed carbs. In some cases, medications like metformin are prescribed.

## Diagnostic Measurements

### Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR)

It's a calculated score (not a separate blood test) derived from two fasting lab
values.

> HOMA-IR = (Fasting Insulin [µIU/mL] × Fasting Glucose [mg/dL]) ÷ 405

**Example:** Fasting insulin 12 µIU/mL, fasting glucose 95 mg/dL → (12 × 95) ÷
405 = **2.8**

**Interpretation:**

| Score   | Meaning                                                     |
|---------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| < 1.0   | High insulin sensitivity (optimal)                          |
| 1.0–1.9 | Normal / favorable                                          |
| 2.0–2.9 | Early insulin resistance                                    |
| 3.0–4.9 | Elevated insulin resistance (higher cardiometabolic risk)   |
| ≥ 5.0   | Marked insulin resistance (metabolic syndrome / T2DM range) |

#### Why It Matters

It detects insulin resistance **years before** fasting glucose or HbA1c cross
into the prediabetes/diabetes range. The 405 divisor was calibrated so that a
perfectly insulin-sensitive person (insulin ~5 µIU/mL, glucose ~90 mg/dL) scores
approximately 1.0.

#### How to Get It

Ask your doctor to order a **fasting insulin** alongside your routine fasting
glucose (many standard metabolic panels don't include insulin). Both must come
from the same 8 to 12 hour fasting draw. Once you have both numbers, the
calculation is trivial, or your lab may compute it for you.

Tracking HOMA-IR before and after an intervention using (diet + supplements +
exercise) is the single best way to confirm that insulin sensitivity is actually
improving, independent of weight loss.

### Lipoprotein Insulin Resistance (LP-IR)

Lipoprotein Insulin Resistance (LP-IR) is a composite blood-test score
(0–100) derived from **NMR lipoprotein particle analysis** that quantifies how
resistant your body is to insulin — without directly measuring insulin. It
captures the downstream lipid-particle changes that chronic insulin resistance
creates, often **years before** glucose or A1c cross diagnostic thresholds.

#### How It Works

Insulin resistance shifts the lipoprotein profile toward more
atherogenic particles. The LP-IR score is a weighted combination of **six
NMR-measured lipoprotein variables** — notably higher large VLDL particles (
VLDL-P), higher small dense LDL particles (small LDL-P), and lower large HDL
particles (HDL-P). A score of **0 = most insulin sensitive**, **100 = most
insulin resistant**.

**Interpreting the score:**

| Range     | Interpretation                                                               |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **< 45**  | Below the clinical threshold for insulin resistance (low)                    |
| **45–48** | Borderline / intermediate                                                    |
| **≥ 48**  | Elevated insulin resistance (some labs use **≥ 68** as a higher-risk cutoff) |

#### Why It Matters

- It predicts **incident type 2 diabetes** robustly and largely **independently
  of glucose, HbA1c, and HOMA-IR** — in one 20-year Women's Health Study
  follow-up, and in the PREVEND cohort (highest vs. lowest quartile HR ≈ 10.18
  for diabetes).
- It can detect insulin resistance even in **normal-weight** people and those
  with **normal glucose**, during the compensatory hyperinsulinemia phase.
- It reflects both **hepatic and peripheral (adipose/muscle) insulin resistance
  **, whereas HOMA-IR leans more toward hepatic.

**vs. HOMA-IR / fasting insulin:** HOMA-IR is calculated from fasting
glucose and insulin and has high day-to-day variability; fasting insulin
fluctuates with stress and acute illness. LP-IR uses a multi-marker lipoprotein
composite, making it **more stable and reproducible** for individual risk
assessment.

**Practical note:** The test requires an **8-hour fast**. It's best used as a
**trend marker** alongside other metabolic labs, with retesting in ~8–12 weeks
to track whether lifestyle changes (diet, activity, sleep) are working.

#### Lifestyle Changes That Lower LP-IR

The LP-IR score is **highly responsive** to lifestyle intervention — meaningful
drops (8–15 points) are achievable within **8–12 weeks** of consistent changes.

##### Most Effective (Strongest Evidence)

| Intervention                                            | Typical LP-IR Reduction                  | Key Evidence                                                |
|---------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Carbohydrate restriction** (especially refined carbs) | ~10 points in 12 wks                     | RCT (N=262): low-carb (−10.3) vs. low-fat (−2.4), P < 0.001 |
| **Resistance training** (2+ sessions/wk)                | 4–12 pts over 6–8 mo                     | STRRIDE trials; most effective single exercise modality     |
| **Aerobic exercise** (150 min/wk moderate)              | ~12 pts (moderate intensity, low volume) | Meta-analysis of 18 RCTs; SMD 0.48                          |
| **Weight loss** (5–10% body weight)                     | ~4–6 pts per 5% lost                     | 10-wk study: −12.1 pts with −8.9% body weight               |
| **Aerobic + dietary combo**                             | ~12–13 pts                               | STRRIDE-PD: combining both was most robust (−12.4 pts)      |

##### Also Important

- **Sleep** — Target **7–9 hours** of quality sleep. Even one week of sleep
  restriction worsens insulin resistance acutely.
- **Stress reduction** — Mindfulness, yoga, or time in nature; chronic stress
  elevates cortisol and worsens insulin sensitivity.
- **Time-restricted eating** (8–10 hr window) — Emerging evidence for reducing
  chronic insulin load.
- **Reduce ultra-processed foods & sugary beverages** — Drives postprandial VLDL
  secretion, the single largest contributor to the LP-IR formula.
- **Increase soluble fiber** (legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains) — Slows
  glucose absorption, blunts insulin spikes.

##### Practical Priority Order

1. **Cut refined carbs & ultra-processed foods** → fastest dietary impact
2. **Add resistance training** 2–3×/week → most effective exercise lever
3. **Add 150 min/wk aerobic activity** (brisk walking, cycling)
4. **Lose 5–10% body weight** if overweight (visceral fat reduction has the
   greatest effect on the VLDL component)
5. **Fix sleep** (7–9 hrs) and **manage chronic stress**

The STRRIDE data is particularly instructive: **exercise alone** produced modest
LP-IR changes, but **exercise combined with dietary intervention and weight loss
** produced the largest and most consistent reductions — suggesting the
interventions work synergistically.

**STRRIDE** stands for **Studies of a Targeted Risk Reduction Intervention
through Defined Exercise**, a series of three randomized controlled trials
conducted at Duke University that together tested **10 different exercise (and
lifestyle) interventions** in 503 adults over 6–8 months. The primary 2021 
paper is: *Frontiers in Physiology* (2021), doi: 10.3389/fphys.2021.626142.
