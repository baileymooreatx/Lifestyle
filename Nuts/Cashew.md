# Cashews

Random fact: The cashew grows outside a "cashew apple" and contains toxic oils 
similar to poison ivy in its shell, requiring careful processing.

A small handful (≈1 oz or 18 nuts) of raw or dry-roasted, unsalted cashews daily 
is a nutrient-dense snack. Portion control is the main consideration.  

## Pros

- **Heart-healthy fats**: Rich in monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fats that
  help lower LDL cholesterol and support cardiovascular health.
- **Plant-based protein**: ~5 g per ounce, making them a solid option for
  vegetarians.
- **Essential minerals**: Excellent source of **copper**, **magnesium**, **zinc
  **, **iron**, and **phosphorus** — supporting energy production, immune
  function, bone health, and nerve function.
- **Antioxidants**: Contain polyphenols, carotenoids, and selenium that help
  neutralize free radicals and reduce oxidative stress.
- **Low in sugar**: Friendly for blood sugar management, including for those
  with type 2 diabetes or insulin resistance.
- **Satiety**: The combination of protein, fat, and fiber helps keep you full,
  supporting weight management when portioned.

## Cons

- **Calorie-dense**: ~157–171 kcal per ounce (≈28 g); easy to overeat, which can
  lead to weight gain.
- **Antinutrients**: Contain **phytic acid**, which can reduce absorption of
  iron, zinc, and calcium (soaking or roasting helps).
- **High oxalate content**: May contribute to kidney stone formation in
  susceptible individuals.
- **Tree nut allergen**: Can trigger severe allergic reactions; cross-reactivity
  with pistachios is common.
- **Lower fiber / higher carbs** than most other nuts (e.g., almonds, walnuts),
  so they're less ideal for strict low-fiber or very low-carb diets.
- **Processed varieties**: Salted, honey-roasted, or oil-roasted cashews add
  sodium, sugar, and unhealthy fats.
