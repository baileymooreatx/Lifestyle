# Alkaline Water

Alkaline water is defined by a higher pH level than regular drinking water.
While standard tap water typically has a neutral pH of 7, alkaline water
generally measures between **pH 8 and 9**. This increased alkalinity is achieved
either naturally as water passes over rocks and picks up minerals like calcium,
magnesium, and potassium, or artificially through electrolysis using an ionizer.
Proponents argue that this chemical difference allows the water to neutralize
excess acid in the bloodstream, although the human body tightly regulates its
own blood pH between 7.35 and 7.45 regardless of dietary intake.

## Claimed Hydration and Athletic Benefits

One of the most frequently cited advantages of alkaline water is **superior
hydration**, particularly for athletes. Advocates claim that alkaline water has
a smaller molecular cluster size, allowing cells to absorb it more rapidly than
regular water.

A 2016 study involving 100 adults found that consuming high-pH water after
strenuous exercise resulted in a **6.3% reduction in blood viscosity**, compared
to a 3.36% reduction with regular water. This suggests that alkaline water may
improve blood flow and oxygen delivery throughout the body more effectively
during rehydration. Additionally, some research indicates it may help buffer 
**lactic acid** buildup during high-intensity workouts, potentially reducing
muscle fatigue and enhancing recovery times.

## Digestive Health and Acid Reflux

Alkaline water is often promoted as a natural remedy for **acid reflux** and
gastrointestinal discomfort. The primary mechanism proposed is the water's
ability to neutralize stomach acid and deactivate **pepsin**, an enzyme that
contributes to tissue damage in acid reflux disease.

Laboratory studies have shown that water with a pH of 8.8 can permanently
inactivate pepsin. Consequently, individuals suffering from gastroesophageal
reflux disease (GERD) or gastric hyperacidity may experience symptom relief,
such as reduced bloating and heartburn, by drinking alkaline water. It is also
suggested that the water helps balance gut acidity, potentially creating a more
favorable environment for beneficial gut bacteria and improving overall nutrient
absorption.

## Bone Health and Metabolic Markers

Some proponents suggest that alkaline water can support **bone density** and
prevent osteoporosis. The theory posits that an acidic diet causes the body to
leach calcium from bones to neutralize the acid; drinking alkaline water
purportedly reduces this acid load, preserving bone mineral content. A study
involving postmenopausal women indicated that those who consumed alkaline water
saw significant improvements in spinal bone density compared to those who drank
regular water.

Regarding metabolic health, limited research has explored effects on **blood
sugar, cholesterol, and blood pressure**. A 2022 study observed that
postmenopausal women who drank at least one liter of alkaline water daily for
two months experienced slight reductions in blood sugar, triglycerides, and
waist circumference. However, these metabolic benefits are not yet universally
accepted, and the water is not considered a standalone treatment for chronic
conditions like diabetes or hypertension.

## Scientific Consensus and Limitations

Despite the numerous claimed benefits, the **scientific consensus remains
cautious**. Major health organizations, including the Mayo Clinic and Harvard
Health, emphasize that robust, large-scale clinical trials are lacking. The
human body possesses highly efficient mechanisms in the lungs and kidneys to
maintain blood pH, meaning drinking alkaline water does not significantly alter
the body's overall acidity.

While small studies show promise for specific issues like acid reflux and
post-exercise hydration, many broader claims—such as anti-aging properties,
cancer prevention, and weight loss—are **not supported by rigorous evidence**.
The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has even banned specific health
claims regarding alkaline water and bone health due to insufficient proof.
Experts generally agree that while alkaline water is safe for most people to
drink, regular water is sufficient for hydration and overall health for the
general population.

## Potential Risks of Drinking Alkaline Water

While alkaline water is generally considered safe for healthy individuals,
specific risks arise from excessive consumption, pre-existing health conditions,
and interactions with medications.

### Kidney Strain and Electrolyte Imbalance

The most significant risks are associated with individuals who have compromised
kidney function. The kidneys are responsible for filtering excess minerals and
regulating the body's pH balance. For people with chronic kidney disease (CKD),
the added minerals often found in alkaline water—such as calcium, magnesium, and
potassium—can accumulate to toxic levels because the kidneys cannot filter them
efficiently. This can lead to **electrolyte imbalances** and place additional
strain on already impaired organs.

Even in healthy individuals, consuming water with a pH higher than 9.8 has been
linked to **hyperkalemia** (excessively high potassium levels in the blood), a
condition that can disrupt heart rhythm and muscle function. Furthermore, while
rare in healthy people, extreme overconsumption could theoretically contribute
to **metabolic alkalosis**, a condition characterized by nausea, vomiting,
muscle twitching, and confusion, though the body's natural regulatory mechanisms
usually prevent this.

### Digestive Disruption and Nutrient Absorption

Stomach acid is essential not only for breaking down food but also for killing
harmful pathogens and bacteria ingested with meals. Regularly drinking large
quantities of highly alkaline water can **neutralize stomach acid**, potentially
lowering the stomach's natural defense against infections. This alteration in pH
may also interfere with the digestion and absorption of certain nutrients that
require an acidic environment to be properly processed. Long-term suppression of
stomach acidity can lead to gastrointestinal issues, including indigestion and
an increased susceptibility to foodborne illnesses.

### Medication Interactions

A critical and often overlooked risk involves the interaction between alkaline
water and prescription medications. Recent research indicates that the high pH
of alkaline water can weaken or dissolve the **protective enteric coatings** on
certain pills before they reach the intestines. These coatings are designed to
prevent the medication from being destroyed by stomach acid or to ensure a slow,
time-released absorption.

If taken with alkaline water, these medications may release **too early** in the
digestive tract, rendering them less effective or causing unintended side
effects. This is particularly concerning for **time-release medications** and
drugs that require a specific acidic environment for proper activation. Experts
recommend taking all medications with plain, neutral water and waiting at least
30 minutes to two hours before consuming alkaline water to avoid these
interactions.

### Lack of Long-Term Safety Data

While short-term studies suggest safety for healthy adults, there is a notable
lack of robust, long-term clinical trials regarding the daily consumption of
alkaline water over years or decades. Some experts caution that continually
altering the body's natural pH balance, even slightly, could have unforeseen
consequences on metabolic processes and organ function over a lifetime.
Consequently, health organizations often advise that for the general population,
the potential risks and costs outweigh the unproven benefits compared to regular
drinking water.
