# Black-eyed Peas

## Nutritional Profile

Here's the nutritional profile for **1 cup (171 g) of cooked, boiled black-eyed
peas** (USDA data):

| **Nutrient**  | **Amount** |  **% Daily Value** |
|---------------|------------|-------------------:|
| Calories      | 198        |                10% |
| Protein       | 13.2 g     |                26% |
| Total Fat     | 0.9 g      |                 1% |
| Carbohydrates | 35.5 g     |                13% |
| Dietary Fiber | 11.1 g     |                40% |
| Sugars        | 5.6 g      |                11% |
| Folate        | 177 µg     |                89% |
| Copper        | 0.9 mg     |                51% |
| Iron          | 4.2 mg     |                24% |
| Thiamin (B1)  | 0.38 mg    |                29% |
| Magnesium     | 79 mg      |                20% |
| Potassium     | 555 mg     |                15% |

**Notable characteristics:**

- Very low in fat and cholesterol-free
- High fiber content (40% DV per serving), which supports digestive health and
  helps lower cholesterol
- A strong plant-based protein source (~13 g per cup)
- Naturally low in sodium
- Moderate glycemic index (~52)  


Here are the key **health benefits** of black-eyed peas.

## Heart Health

The soluble fiber binds to bile acids and cholesterol in the digestive tract,
helping lower LDL ("bad") cholesterol. A 12-week randomized trial found
black-eyed pea powder improved both HDL and LDL cholesterol. Their high
potassium (~478 mg/cup) combined with very low sodium (~7 mg/cup) also supports
healthy blood pressure.

## Blood Sugar & Diabetes

With a **low glycemic index (~52)**, the combination of protein, fiber, and
resistant starch produces a slow, steady glucose release rather than a spike.
This makes them a useful staple for managing blood sugar and reducing type 2
diabetes risk.

## Digestive Health

The 11 g of fiber per cup acts as a **prebiotic**, feeding beneficial gut
bacteria and promoting a healthy microbiome. It also supports regularity,
reduces constipation, and may lower the risk of colorectal issues.

## Weight Management

The protein (~13 g/cup) suppresses ghrelin (the hunger hormone), while the
soluble fiber forms a gel in the gut that slows digestion and keeps you full
longer. A study of 1,475 adults found regular bean eaters had a **23% lower risk
of increased belly fat**.

## Folate & Pregnancy

One cup provides **~89% of the daily folate value**, a critical nutrient for
preventing neural tube defects in developing fetuses.

## Other Notable Benefits

- **Iron** (~24% DV) helps prevent anemia, especially for plant-based eaters
- **Magnesium** (~20% DV) supports over 300 biochemical reactions, including
  muscle/nerve function and energy production
- **Manganese** supports cellular energy production and acts as an antioxidant
- **Vitamin A** (~13% DV per half cup) supports eye health
- **Polyphenols and flavonoids** provide antioxidant protection against cell
  damage

The main caveat: the iron is non-heme, so pairing black-eyed peas with a vitamin
C source (like lemon juice or tomatoes) significantly improves absorption.
