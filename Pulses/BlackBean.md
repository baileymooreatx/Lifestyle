# Black Beans

## Nutritional Profile

Here's the nutritional profile for **1 cup (172 g) of cooked, boiled black
beans, without salt** (USDA data).

| **Nutrient**  | **Amount** | **% Daily Value** |
|---------------|------------|------------------:|
| Calories      | 227        |               11% |
| Protein       | 15.2 g     |               30% |
| Total Fat     | 0.9 g      |                1% |
| Carbohydrates | 40.8 g     |               15% |
| Dietary Fiber | 15.0 g     |               53% |
| Sugars        | 0.6 g      |                1% |
| Folate        | 256 µg     |               64% |
| Magnesium     | 120 mg     |               29% |
| Manganese     | 1.0 mg     |               33% |
| Thiamin (B1)  | 0.4 mg     |               35% |
| Phosphorus    | 241 mg     |               34% |
| Copper        | 0.4 mg     |               40% |
| Iron          | 3.6 mg     |               20% |
| Potassium     | 611 mg     |               13% |
| Zinc          | 2.0 mg     |               18% |
| Vitamin B6    | 0.1 mg     |                9% |

**Notable characteristics:**

- Very low in sugar (~0.6 g per cup), among the lowest of any common legume
- High fiber content per serving (53% DV)
- Strong plant-based protein source (~15 g per cup)
- Very low glycemic index (~30), making them excellent for blood sugar
  management
- Naturally low in sodium (~2 mg per cup when cooked without salt)
- Cooks in about 60 minutes (or 25 min in a pressure cooker); soaking overnight
  reduces cooking time to ~30 min

Here are the key **health benefits** of black beans.

## Anti-Inflammatory

In a 12-week study of 72 adults with prediabetes (presented at NUTRITION 2025,
American Society for Nutrition), daily black bean consumption significantly
reduced **interleukin-6 (IL-6)**, a key pro-inflammatory cytokine, from 2.57 to
1.88 pg/mL. Chronic low-grade inflammation is a major driver of heart disease
and type 2 diabetes, making this a particularly meaningful finding for at-risk
populations.

## Heart Health & Cholesterol

A 4-week randomized crossover study found that eating 1 cup of canned beans
(including black beans) daily **decreased both total and LDL cholesterol** in
adults with elevated LDL. A separate study in the *Journal of the American
College of Nutrition* reported that just a half-cup of beans daily dropped total
and LDL cholesterol by **more than 8%** — compared to 2–3% from a half-cup of
oatmeal. Black beans also contain **saponins and quercetin**, which have
independent cholesterol-lowering and anti-atherosclerotic properties. In a 
small randomized trial, participants' **blood pressures were lower** after 
eating black beans, attributed to improved blood vessel relaxation.

## Blood Sugar & Insulin Resistance

With a **very low glycemic index (~30)**, black beans produce a slow, steady
glucose release. A USDA Agricultural Research Service study found that adding
black beans to a high-fat diet in obese mice **improved insulin sensitivity**,
reduced LDL cholesterol by 28%, and lowered triglycerides by 37%. The saponins
in black beans also help the body respond to glucose in a more beneficial way.

## Gut Health & Microbiome

The 15 g of fiber per cup (including resistant starch) feeds beneficial gut
bacteria and stimulates production of **short-chain fatty acids** like butyrate.
The USDA Agricultural Research Service study found black beans **restored 
the balance of gut bacteria** in obese mice, decreasing the 
Firmicutes-to-Bacteroidetes ratio by 64% and significantly reducing inflammatory 
bacteria such as *Blautia* and *Clostridium*.

The **Firmicutes-to-Bacteroidetes (F/B) ratio** compares the relative abundance 
of the two most abundant bacterial phyla in the human gut.

* Firmicutes – more efficient at fermenting complex carbohydrates and extracting 
  calories from fiber
* Bacteroidetes – tend to dominate in leaner individuals and are associated 
  with less aggressive calorie extraction

A higher F/B ratio (Firmicutes dominance) has been linked in research to 
obesity, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and low-grade inflammation. The 
proposed mechanism is that a Firmicutes-rich microbiome harvests more energy 
from the same diet, promoting fat storage.

## Weight Management

The combination of ~15 g protein and 15 g fiber per cup promotes strong satiety.
Research consistently shows that regular bean consumers have **lower body fat
and smaller waist circumference** compared to non-consumers, likely due to the
combined effects of satiety, low glycemic index, and improved gut microbiome.

## Cancer Risk

A study from Michigan State University published in the *Journal of Nutrition
and Cancer* found that rats fed black beans had a **75% reduction in colon
cancer** compared to the control group, attributed to the high antioxidant and
fiber content.

## Other Notable Benefits

- **Magnesium** (~29% DV) supports over 300 biochemical reactions, including
  muscle/nerve function and energy production
- **Folate** (~64% DV) is critical for DNA synthesis, cell division, and neural
  tube development during pregnancy
- **Iron** (~20% DV) helps prevent anemia — pair with vitamin C for better
  absorption (non-heme iron)
- **Potassium** (~611 mg/cup) combined with very low sodium supports healthy
  blood pressure
- **Polyphenols and flavonoids** provide antioxidant protection against
  oxidative stress and cellular damage

## Caveats  

- As with other legumes, the iron is [non-heme](./NonHemeIron.md), so pairing
  black beans with a vitamin C source (lemon juice, tomatoes, peppers)
  significantly improves absorption. 
- Canned black beans can be high in sodium (~300+ mg per half cup), so draining 
  and rinsing helps.
