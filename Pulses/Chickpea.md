# Chickpeas (Garbanzo Beans)

## Nutritional Profile

Nutritional profile for **1 cup (164 g) of cooked, boiled chickpeas** (USDA
data).

| **Nutrient**  | **Amount** | **% Daily Value** |
|---------------|------------|------------------:|
| Calories      | 269        |               13% |
| Protein       | 14.5 g     |               29% |
| Total Fat     | 4.2 g      |                5% |
| Carbohydrates | 45 g       |               16% |
| Dietary Fiber | 12.5 g     |               45% |
| Sugars        | 7.9 g      |               16% |
| Folate        | 282 µg     |               71% |
| Manganese     | 1.7 mg     |               73% |
| Copper        | 1.0 mg     |               64% |
| Iron          | 4.7 mg     |               26% |
| Phosphorus    | 276 mg     |               39% |
| Zinc          | 3.0 mg     |               23% |
| Magnesium     | 79 mg      |               19% |
| Thiamin (B1)  | 0.2 mg     |               16% |
| Potassium     | 477 mg     |               10% |

**Notable characteristics:**

- Exceptionally high in **manganese** (73% DV) and **copper** (64% DV) — both
  support metabolism and antioxidant defense
- Strong folate source (~71% DV), important for cell division and pregnancy
- Very low glycemic index (~28), making it one of the slowest-digesting common
  legumes
- Naturally low in sodium (~11 mg per cup when cooked without salt)

Here are the key **health benefits** of chickpeas.

## Heart Health

In a randomized crossover trial of 47 adults, eating chickpeas daily for at
least five weeks lowered **total cholesterol by 3.9%** and **LDL by 4.6%**
compared to a wheat-supplemented diet. A separate 12-week study of 45 adults
found that incorporating chickpeas into the diet reduced total cholesterol,
fasting insulin, and HOMA-IR (a marker of insulin resistance). Their
potassium (~477 mg/cup) and very low sodium also support healthy blood pressure.

## Blood Sugar & Diabetes

With a **very low glycemic index (~28)** — one of the lowest of any common
food — chickpeas produce a slow, steady glucose release. A review of 28 studies
found they produced **significantly lower post-meal blood sugar spikes** than
similar carbohydrate foods. A meta-analysis of 12 studies (182 adults) confirmed
chickpeas were more effective than potatoes or wheat at blunting after-meal
glucose spikes, attributed to their high protein, fiber, and resistant starch
content.

## Gut Health & Microbiome

The 12.5 g of fiber per cup (including resistant starch) feeds beneficial gut
bacteria and stimulates production of **short-chain fatty acids** like butyrate,
which supports intestinal barrier integrity, reduces inflammation, and may lower
colorectal cancer risk. Chickpeas also contain alpha-galactosidase, which has
been shown to increase *Bifidobacteria* and *Lactobacilli* levels in the gut.

## Weight Management

The combination of ~14.5 g protein and 12.5 g fiber per cup promotes strong
satiety. A 12-week ad libitum feeding study found that participants eating
chickpeas consumed more polyunsaturated fat and fiber overall and showed
improved lipid and glycemic profiles without intentional caloric restriction.

## Bone & Connective Tissue

One cup provides **73% of the daily manganese value**, a mineral essential for
bone formation, connective tissue synthesis, and the function of antioxidant
enzymes like superoxide dismutase.

## Folate & Cell Division

At ~71% DV per cup, chickpeas are a strong folate source, critical for DNA
synthesis, cell division, and neural tube development during pregnancy.

## Other Notable Benefits

- **Copper** (~64% DV) supports iron metabolism and antioxidant defense
- **Iron** (~26% DV) helps prevent anemia — pair with vitamin C for better
  absorption (non-heme iron)
- **Phosphorus** (~39% DV) supports bone mineralization and energy metabolism
- **Polyphenols and bioactive peptides** provide antioxidant and
  anti-inflammatory activity

## Caveats

- As with other legumes, the iron is [non-heme](./NonHemeIron.md), so pairing
  chickpeas with a vitamin C source (lemon juice, tomatoes, peppers)
  significantly improves absorption.
- Canned chickpeas can be high in sodium (~300+ mg per half
cup), so draining and rinsing helps.
