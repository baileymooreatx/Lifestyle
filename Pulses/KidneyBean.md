## Blood Sugar Control  

The combination of fiber, protein, and resistant starch gives kidney beans a 
**low glycemic index** (~32–53). This slows glucose absorption after meals,
making them a strong food choice for managing type 2 diabetes or insulin
resistance.

## Heart Health & Cholesterol  

The soluble fiber binds cholesterol in the gut and reduces its absorption,
lowering **LDL ("bad") cholesterol** while leaving HDL intact. The potassium and
magnesium content also supports healthy blood pressure.

## Weight Management  

At ~225 kcal per cup with high protein (15 g) and fiber (11+ g), kidney beans
promote **satiety** and reduce overall calorie intake. The protein also contains
starch-digesting inhibitors that further slow carb absorption.

## Gut & Colon Health  

The fiber and resistant starch act as **prebiotics**, feeding beneficial gut
bacteria. Fermentation produces short-chain fatty acids (butyrate, propionate)
that nourish colon cells and may reduce the risk of **colorectal cancer**.

## Rich in Micronutrients  

- **Folate** (~58% DV): critical for red blood cell production, especially
  important during pregnancy
- **Iron** (~29% DV): supports oxygen transport; helps prevent anemia
- **Magnesium** (~19% DV): supports nerve/muscle function and blood pressure
- **Manganese** (~22% DV): involved in bone development and metabolism
- **Potassium** (~15% DV): electrolyte balance and heart function

## Antioxidant & Anti-inflammatory Effects
Kidney beans contain **phenolic compounds and anthocyanins** (especially in red
varieties) that neutralize free radicals, reduce oxidative stress, and may lower
chronic inflammation.

## Caveats

- Raw or undercooked kidney beans contain **phytohaemagglutinin**, a toxic 
  lectin. Always boil them thoroughly (at least 10 minutes) before eating.
- As with other legumes, the iron is [non-heme](./NonHemeIron.md), so pairing
  kidney beans with a vitamin C source (lemon juice, tomatoes, peppers)
  significantly improves absorption.
- Kidney beans contain **phytates** (phytic acid), which bind to the iron and 
  make it even less available. So the actual amount of iron you absorb from a 
  cup of kidney beans is considerably lower than the 5.2 mg on the label 
  suggests.
