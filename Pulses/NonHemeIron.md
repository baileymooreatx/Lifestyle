# Non-heme Iron

All iron in plants is **non-heme iron**, which is absorbed at only **2 to 20%
efficiency** (vs. ~15–35% for heme iron from meat).

## How Vitamin C Helps

Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) works through two mechanisms.  

1. **Reduction** — converts ferric iron (Fe³⁺) to the more absorbable ferrous
   form (Fe²⁺)
2. **Chelation** — forms a soluble complex that keeps iron from precipitating in
   the alkaline intestinal environment, and directly counteracts phytate binding

A meta-analysis of 26 studies found vitamin C raises non-heme iron absorption by
a mean of **~6 percentage points**, and older single-meal studies show **2–3×
increases** with 25–100 mg of vitamin C at the same meal.

## Sources  

| Source                       | Vitamin C |
|------------------------------|-----------|
| Squeeze of lemon/lime        | ~30–50 mg |
| Half a bell pepper           | ~45 mg    |
| Sliced tomato / tomato sauce | ~15–25 mg |
| Side of strawberries         | ~50–80 mg |

## What to Avoid at the Same Meal

- **Tea and coffee** — polyphenols/tannins inhibit non-heme iron absorption
- **Calcium-rich foods** (dairy, supplements) — the only inhibitor that affects
  both heme *and* non-heme iron
- **High-dose zinc supplements**

## Bonus: Soaking & Cooking

Soaking dried beans before cooking (and discarding the soak water) reduces
phytate content, which further improves iron bioavailability. This stacks with
the vitamin C effect.
