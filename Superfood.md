# What is a "superfood?"

Superfood is a marketing and media term used to describe foods that offer
maximum nutritional benefits for minimal calories. While there is no official
scientific or legal definition recognized by regulatory bodies like the U.S.
Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or the European Food Safety Authority, the
term generally refers to nutrient-dense foods rich in vitamins, minerals,
antioxidants, fiber, and healthy fatty acids.

## Key Characteristics

* **Nutrient Density**: These foods provide high levels of essential nutrients
  relative to their calorie count.
* **Health Claims**: They are often promoted for specific health benefits, such
  as reducing oxidative stress, supporting heart health, or boosting the immune
  system.
* **Marketing Origin**: The term was originally coined in the early 20th century
  by the United Fruit Company to market bananas. It has since become a
  widespread tool for food industry marketing, often applied to exotic or "
  ancient" grains and berries to create consumer demand and justify higher
  prices.

## Common Examples

While lists vary, common foods labeled as superfoods include:

* **Berries**: [Blueberries](./Fruits/Blueberry.md),
  [strawberries](./Fruits/Strawberry.md), goji berries, and acai berries.
* **Leafy Greens**: Kale, [spinach](./Vegetables/Spinach.md), and Swiss chard.
* **Fatty Fish**: Salmon and sardines (rich in Omega-3s).
* **Nuts and Seeds**: [Chia seeds](./Seeds/Chia.md),
  [flaxseeds](./Seeds/Flaxseed.md), [hemp seeds](./Seeds/HempHearts.md), and
  [walnuts](./Nuts/Walnut.md).
* **Pulses and Grains**: Quinoa, [lentils](./Pulses/Lentil.md), and chickpeas.

## Scientific Perspective

Nutritionists and dietitians emphasize that "superfood" is not a distinct
nutritional category. No single food can prevent disease or compensate for a
poor diet. Health experts recommend a balanced diet that includes a variety of
whole foods rather than focusing on specific "super" items. In 2007, the
European Union restricted the use of the term "superfood" on packaging unless
accompanied by specific, scientifically proven health claims.
