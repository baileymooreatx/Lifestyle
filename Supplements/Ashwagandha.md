# Ashwagandha

Ashwagandha (*Withania somnifera*) is an evergreen shrub native to India,
Africa, and the Middle East, used for thousands of years in **Ayurvedic
medicine**. Also called "Indian ginseng" or "winter cherry," it's classified as
an **adaptogen**, a substance that helps the body cope with stress. The name
means "smell of the horse" in Sanskrit, referring to its root's scent.  

Its key bioactive compounds include **withanolides** (steroidal lactones),
alkaloids, flavonoids, and various antioxidants.  

Ashwagandha is generally considered safe for short-term use (up to 3 months),
though long-term safety data is limited. Common side effects include drowsiness
and mild stomach upset.  

## Evidence-Based Benefits

| Benefit                        | Notes                                            |
|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| **Stress & anxiety reduction** | Lowers cortisol; most well-studied benefit       |
| **Improved sleep**             | Sedative effects support restful sleep           |
| **Reduced inflammation**       | Withanolides inhibit inflammatory markers        |
| **Blood sugar management**     | May lower HbA1c, insulin, and blood lipids       |
| **Brain function**             | May improve memory, reaction time, and cognition |
| **Athletic performance**       | Increased strength, endurance, and oxygen use    |
| **Immune support**             | Antioxidant and immunomodulatory properties      |
| **Male fertility**             | Improved sperm quality and testosterone          |

than from vitamins or minerals.

## Brands

KSM-66 and Sensoril are **branded, patented ashwagandha extracts**, the
most common supplements. Both are standardized to specific levels of
withanolides, but they differ in source, potency, and research profile.

|                     | **KSM-66**                                 | **Sensoril**                                      |
|---------------------|--------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| **Maker**           | Ixoreal Biomed (India)                     | Kerry Group / Natreon (US)                        |
| **Plant part**      | Root **only**                              | Root **+ leaf**                                   |
| **Standardized to** | 5% withanolides                            | 10% withanolide glycosides + 32% oligosaccharides |
| **Extraction**      | Proprietary "green chemistry," no solvents | Aqueous (water-based), no solvents                |
| **Clinical trials** | 24–30+ double-blind RCTs \*                | 12+ published studies                             |
| **Typical dose**    | 300–600 mg/day                             | 125–250 mg/day                                    |
| **Best for**        | Stress, athletic performance, cognition    | Stress, sleep, cognitive support                  |
| **Available since** | ~2006                                      | ~2004                                             |

\* RCT is Randomized Controlled Trial, the gold standard of clinical research
design. In an RCT, participants are randomly assigned to either the treatment
group (e.g., KSM-66) or a control group (e.g., placebo), and both groups are
typically blinded to which they received. "Double-blind" means neither the
participants nor the researchers know who's in which group, which eliminates
bias. This is why KSM-66's 24–30+ RCTs make it the better-evidenced extract.
The results are less likely to be artifacts of placebo effect, selection bias,
or researcher bias.

### Key practical differences

- **Potency per mg:** Sensoril is ~2× more concentrated in withanolides, so you
  take half the dose.
- **Profile:** KSM-66 preserves the full root spectrum (alkaloids, saponins,
  withanolides); Sensoril captures additional leaf-derived bioactives like
  **withaferin A** and **oligosaccharides**.
- **Evidence base:** KSM-66 has roughly **double** the number of human clinical
  trials, making it the more "proven" option for stress, strength, and sleep
  outcomes.
- **Sedation:** Sensoril tends to feel slightly more sedating, which is why it's
  often recommended for evening use.

**KSM-66** = root-only, lower concentration, more research.

**Sensoril** = root + leaf, higher concentration, slightly more sedating.

Both are legitimate, well-made extracts — the choice mostly comes down to
whether you want the larger evidence base (KSM-66) or the higher potency per mg
(Sensoril).

## Dosage

| Extract      | Withanolides       | Typical Dose       | Notes                                  |
|--------------|--------------------|--------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **KSM-66**   | ~5% (root only)    | **300–600 mg/day** | Most studied; usually 300 mg × 2 doses |
| **Sensoril** | ~10% (root + leaf) | **125–250 mg/day** | More sedating; good for evening/sleep  |
| **Shoden**   | ~35% (root + leaf) | **120 mg/day**     | Highest concentration; smallest dose   |

**Start low:** Take 300 mg KSM-66 (or 125 mg Sensoril) once daily for 1 to 2 
weeks to assess tolerance, then increase to the full target dose if no GI upset 
or drowsiness.

## Timing

- **Morning** — waking anxiety, daytime stress
- **Evening** (with last meal) — sleep support
- **Split** (AM + PM) — general cortisol management; this is what most trials
  use

## Cycling

No clinical trial has directly compared cycling vs. continuous use, so protocols
are practitioner conventions. The most common patterns:

| Protocol         | On / Off                      | Best For                                                                         |
|------------------|-------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Standard**     | 8–12 weeks on → 2–4 weeks off | Mirrors trial durations; most widely recommended                                 |
| **Weekly**       | 5 days on → 2 days off        | Gentler; avoids a full break                                                     |
| **Conservative** | 2 weeks on → 2 weeks off      | High-dose users; Andrew Huberman suggests this to limit thyroid/hormonal effects |
| **Ayurvedic**    | 3 months on → 1 month off     | Traditional practice                                                             |

**Why cycle?** Potential reasons include avoiding receptor desensitization,
preventing thyroid hormone drift (ashwagandha raises T3/T4), and reducing the
(rare) risk of liver enzyme elevation with prolonged use.

## Protocol Summary

1. Start at 300 mg KSM-66 (or 125 mg Sensoril) once daily for 1–2 weeks
2. Increase to 600 mg KSM-66 (split AM/PM) or 250 mg Sensoril (evening)
3. Use for 8–12 weeks
4. Take 2–4 weeks off
5. Reassess — restart only if you still notice benefit

Higher doses beyond 600 mg/day haven't shown proportionally better results in
trials and increase side effect risk.

## Medication Interactions

Ashwagandha has several notable interactions with commonly prescribed
medications.

| Medication Class                                                       | Interaction Risk                                                           | Why                                                            |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Sedatives / sleep aids** (e.g., Valium, Xanax, Zolpidem)             | **High** — additive CNS depression, excessive drowsiness, slowed breathing | Ashwagandha has sedative and GABAergic effects                 |
| **Diabetes medications** (insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas)           | **Moderate–High** — risk of hypoglycemia (dangerously low blood sugar)     | Ashwagandha independently lowers blood glucose by ~12–18 mg/dL |
| **Blood pressure medications** (lisinopril, amlodipine, beta-blockers) | **Moderate–High** — additive hypotension (dizziness, fainting)             | Ashwagandha lowers BP by ~5–10 mmHg                            |
| **Thyroid medications** (levothyroxine, Armour Thyroid)                | **Moderate** — may push T3/T4 above therapeutic range                      | Ashwagandha increases thyroid hormone levels by ~10–15%        |
| **Immunosuppressants** (cyclosporine, prednisone, methotrexate)        | **Moderate** — may counteract immune suppression                           | Ashwagandha is immune-stimulating                              |
| **Anticonvulsants** (phenobarbital, primidone)                         | **Moderate** — additive CNS depression                                     | Sedative synergy                                               |
| **Blood thinners** (warfarin, aspirin)                                 | **Moderate** — increased bleeding risk                                     | Ashwagandha may enhance anticoagulant effects                  |

**Additional cautions:**

- **Antidepressants** (SSRIs/SNRIs like sertraline or escitalopram) — limited
  data, but a theoretical risk of serotonin-related effects has been flagged.
- **Acetaminophen** — may increase liver injury risk when combined.
- **Alcohol** — additive sedative effect.

If you take any of the above medications, talk to your doctor or pharmacist
before adding ashwagandha. The interactions are dose-dependent, and monitoring
(e.g., blood sugar, BP, TSH) may be needed if you do combine them.
