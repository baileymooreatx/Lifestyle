# Fiber Supplements

Most Americans consume only ~15–17 g of fiber per day, well below the
recommended **25 g (women) to 38 g (men)**. Supplements are a practical way to
close that gap, though a food-first approach is always preferred.

## Main Types

| **Fiber**             | **Source**               | **Soluble / Insoluble**     | **Best Known For**                       |
|:----------------------|:-------------------------|:----------------------------|:-----------------------------------------|
| Psyllium husk         | *Plantago ovata* seed    | Mostly soluble              | Regularity, LDL cholesterol, blood sugar |
| Methylcellulose       | Modified plant cellulose | Soluble, non-fermentable    | Regularity with minimal gas              |
| Wheat dextrin         | Processed wheat starch   | Soluble, non-gelling        | Gentle daily fiber boost                 |
| Inulin / chicory root | Chicory, agave           | Soluble, highly fermentable | Prebiotic / microbiome support           |
| Glucomannan           | Konjac root              | Soluble, very viscous       | Satiety, cholesterol                     |
| Acacia fiber          | Acacia tree sap          | Soluble, gentle             | Sensitive digestion, IBS                 |
| Resistant starch      | Potato, green banana     | Soluble, fermentable        | Butyrate, insulin sensitivity            |
| Cellulose             | Plant cell walls         | Insoluble                   | Bulk, transit time                       |
| Polycarbophil         | Synthetic                | Insoluble, gelling          | Constipation, IBS                        |

## Key Benefits (Evidence-Based)

- **Regularity & constipation** — strongest evidence for psyllium and
  methylcellulose
- **Lower LDL cholesterol** — viscous soluble fibers (psyllium, beta-glucan,
  guar gum); psyllium carries an FDA heart-health claim
- **Blood sugar control** — viscous fibers slow glucose absorption
- **Satiety & weight** — gel-forming fibers increase fullness
- **Colon health** — fermentable fibers ([inulin](./Inulin.md), 
  [resistant starch](./ResistantStarch.md)) produce
  [short-chain fatty acids](../Body/ShortChainFattyAcid.md)  
- **[Blood pressure](../Body/BloodPressure.md)** — ~20 g soluble fiber/day 
  modestly lowers systolic BP

## Practical Tips

- **Start low, go slow** — begin at 3 to 4 g/day and ramp up over 1–2 weeks to
  avoid gas and bloating
- **Take with plenty of water** (at least 8 oz per dose) — especially critical
  for psyllium and glucomannan, which can swell and cause choking or obstruction
  if taken without enough liquid
- **Spread doses throughout the day** rather than taking everything at once
- **Daily use is safe** for healthy adults (per Mayo Clinic); no tolerable upper
  limit is set, but there's little benefit beyond the recommended total
- **Capsules vs. powder** — capsules are convenient but often require many per
  day; powders are more cost-effective for higher doses
- **Food first** — supplements work best alongside fiber-rich whole foods, not
  as a replacement

## Who Might Need One Most?

People with consistently low fruit/veg intake, chronic constipation, elevated
LDL, type 2 diabetes, or those in post-surgical recovery.

## Insulin Sensitivity

Strongest evidence for improving insulin sensitivity:

- **[Resistant starch](./ResistantStarch.md)** — the standout. A 12-week RCT in 
  insulin-resistant individuals found a **19% increase in insulin 
  sensitivity** (vs. a 14% *decrease* in placebo). Other studies report 33–50% 
  improvements after just 4 weeks at 15–30 g/day. The mechanism is direct: 
  [gut bacteria](../Body/Microbiome.md) ferment it into 
  [butyrate](../Body/Butyrate.md), which enhances insulin signaling in muscle 
  and reduces free fatty acids (a key driver of insulin resistance). A 2019 
  meta-analysis confirmed improvements in fasting glucose, fasting insulin, 
  and HOMA-IR.
- **Psyllium husk** — a 2024 meta-analysis of 19 RCTs found significant
  reductions in **HOMA-IR** (insulin resistance marker), fasting blood sugar (~7
  mg/dL), and HbA1c (~0.75 points) at doses above 10 g/day. It works through two
  routes: its viscous gel **slows glucose absorption** (reducing post-meal
  spikes and insulin demand), and partial fermentation produces SCFAs that
  improve insulin signaling.
- **[Inulin](./Inulin.md)** — a study in *Gut* (2019) showed that 
  inulin-propionate ester and high-fermentable fiber improved insulin 
  sensitivity in overweight/obese adults. The mechanism is the same 
  [SCFA](../Body/ShortChainFattyAcid.md)/GLP-1 pathway as resistant starch, but
  inulin's rapid fermentation makes it somewhat less well-tolerated.
- **Oat beta-glucan** — lowers post-meal glucose and raises GLP-1, the incretin
  hormone that stimulates glucose-dependent insulin release.
- **Glucomannan** — forms the thickest gel of any fiber, most strongly slowing
  glucose entry into the bloodstream.
- **Partially hydrolyzed guar gum (PHGG)** — reduced fasting glucose and
  improved insulin resistance markers in T2D patients, linked to increased
  butyrate-producing bacteria.

**The two mechanisms, summarized:**

| **Mechanism**                               | **Fibers**                         | **How it helps insulin sensitivity**                                                        |
|:--------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Viscous gel (slows absorption)              | Psyllium, beta-glucan, glucomannan | Flattens post-meal glucose curve → less insulin needed                                      |
| Fermentation → SCFAs (butyrate, propionate) | Resistant starch, inulin, PHGG     | Butyrate directly enhances insulin signaling; SCFAs stimulate GLP-1 and reduce inflammation |

If insulin sensitivity is your primary goal, **resistant starch** has the most 
direct and impressive evidence, and **psyllium** is the best "dual-mechanism" 
option (both viscous and partially fermentable). The two complement each other 
well. A common approach is psyllium with meals plus a smaller dose of resistant 
starch (e.g., cooled potato starch) between meals.

## Summary of My Fiber Supplements

I use psyllium husk powder, inulin, and potato starch. These three fibers all
work through **different mechanisms**.

|                            | **Psyllium husk**                                   | **Inulin**                              | **Potato starch** (resistant starch)                   |
|:---------------------------|:----------------------------------------------------|:----------------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------|
| **Type**                   | Soluble, gel-forming                                | Soluble, non-gelling                    | Soluble, fermentable                                   |
| **Primary mechanism**      | Traps water, forms viscous gel, bulks stool         | Fermented by gut bacteria → SCFAs       | Fermented in colon → butyrate                          |
| **Fermentability**         | Low (barely fermented)                              | High, rapid                             | High, moderate                                         |
| **Gas / bloating risk**    | Low                                                 | High                                    | Moderate                                               |
| **Best for**               | Constipation, LDL cholesterol, blood sugar, satiety | Microbiome diversity, prebiotic support | Colon health, butyrate production, insulin sensitivity |
| **Typical effective dose** | 10–20 g/day                                         | 5–12 g/day                              | 10–30 g/day                                            |
| **Key caveat**             | Needs adequate water; can thicken in throat         | High-FODMAP; may worsen IBS symptoms    | Must not be heated to retain "resistance"; initial gas |

**Key contrasts in plain terms:**

- **Psyllium** is the *mechanical* fiber — it physically softens and bulks stool
  via its gel, with minimal bacterial fermentation. It has the strongest
  clinical evidence for constipation and the only FDA-recognized soluble-fiber
  heart-health claim.
- **Inulin** is the *prebiotic* fiber — it doesn't gel or bulk; its entire
  benefit comes from being rapidly fermented into short-chain fatty acids that
  feed the microbiome. That same rapid fermentation is why it causes the most
  gas and bloating of the three.
- **Potato starch** sits in between — it's also a prebiotic (ferments to
  butyrate, the colon's preferred fuel), but its fermentation is slower and more
  targeted to the distal colon, so it's generally better tolerated than inulin
  while still supporting microbial diversity.

**Practical takeaway:** 
- Psyllium for regularity and heart health  
- Inulin for microbiome diversity (if you tolerate 
  [FODMAPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FODMAP))  
- Resistant starch for colon health and metabolic support  

They're complementary, not redundant. Many people combine psyllium with a 
smaller dose of one of the fermentable fibers. Start low and ramp up over 1 or 
2 weeks to minimize gas.
