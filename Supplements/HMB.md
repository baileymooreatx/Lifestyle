# HMB (β-hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate)

HMB (β-hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate) is a metabolite of the amino acid leucine,
typically dosed at ~3 g/day. Its main advantages follow, supported by
meta-analyses and systematic reviews.

## Muscle Preservation (Strongest Evidence)

- **Anti-catabolic action** — HMB's core benefit is reducing muscle protein
  breakdown by inhibiting the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, while simultaneously
  stimulating protein synthesis via the mTOR/AKT pathway.
- **Sarcopenia & aging** — A 2025 meta-analysis of 21 RCTs (1,935 participants
  over 50) confirmed that 3 g/day for 12+ weeks significantly improves muscle
  mass, strength, and physical function in older adults. It's considered
  superior to protein supplementation alone for this purpose.
- **Bed rest / immobility** — HMB preserves lean mass during 10 days of enforced
  bed rest in older adults.
- **Caloric restriction & weight loss** — HMB helps maintain muscle mass during
  dieting, and is increasingly studied as a countermeasure to the muscle loss
  associated with GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy (e.g., semaglutide).

## Exercise Performance & Recovery

- **Reduces exercise-induced muscle damage** (lower CK, less
  soreness/stiffness), particularly in untrained individuals and after novel or
  eccentric loading.
- **Increases strength and lean mass** when combined with resistance training in
  untrained subjects (13–18% strength gains in meta-analyses).
- **Improves aerobic performance** in endurance athletes, likely via enhanced
  mitochondrial biogenesis and fat oxidation.
- **Accelerates recovery** post-exercise.

## Other Benefits

- **Anti-inflammatory** — attenuates IL-6-driven atrophy signaling (JAK2/STAT3
  pathway) and reduces inflammatory factor release.
- **Post-surgical recovery** — accelerates wound healing and reduces
  immobilization time after orthopedic surgery.
- **Mitochondrial protection** — preserves mitochondrial function and integrity
  during atrophy.

## Important Caveats

- Benefits are **modest in already-trained athletes** — the evidence for
  augmenting gains in competitive or experienced lifters is mixed to negligible.
- The strongest, most consistent evidence is for **untrained individuals, older
  adults, and catabolic states** (illness, surgery, bed rest, caloric deficit).
- A 2026 study showed HMB added to 40 g whey [protein](../Protein/Protein.md)
  further increased muscle protein synthesis in older *women* but not older men,
  suggesting sex-based differences in responsiveness.

In short, HMB is most valuable as a **muscle-preservation tool** rather than a
growth enhancer — its niche is protecting lean mass when the body is under
catabolic stress.

## Increase Keytone Production

A 2021 mouse study (Ishii et al.) is the most direct evidence: oral HMB (0.215
g/kg/day for 10 days) significantly elevated **β-hydroxybutyrate (BHB)** in both
plasma and skeletal muscle within 1 hour of dosing. The mechanism is not the
standard "HMB → acetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA → BHB" route. Instead, HMB is converted to
**β-methylcrotonyl-CoA (MC-CoA)**, which then serves as a **direct substrate**
for BHB synthesis — the authors explicitly noted that plasma glucose was
unchanged, ruling out a secondary effect of reduced glucose on ketogenesis.

This is consistent with the broader leucine catabolic pathway: leucine → KIC →
isovaleryl-CoA → HMB → MC-CoA → (BHB / acetyl-CoA). So HMB sits at a branch
point where it can feed ketone production directly.

### Caveats

- The evidence is **animal-only** (mice). No human RCT has measured the effect
  of HMB supplementation on circulating ketone levels.
- The magnitude in mice was modest — a statistically significant rise, but not
  enough to produce a clinically meaningful level of ketosis on its own.
- A 2021 MDPI study found the reverse interaction: when BHB is already
  elevated (ketosis), HMB **attenuates** BHB-driven epigenetic changes (lysine
  β-hydroxybutyrylation) in muscle, suggesting HMB may *buffer* against
  ketosis-associated muscle wasting rather than simply add to ketone output.
- A patent (CN116018155A) describes combined "KB-HMB" formulations (HMB +
  exogenous BHB/acetoacetate), claiming synergistic lean-mass benefits, but this
  is not peer-reviewed clinical evidence.

HMB is a genuine (if minor) ketogenic substrate, but taking it is not a
practical way to raise ketone levels. If the goal is ketosis, dietary fat, MCTs,
or exogenous ketone salts are far more effective. HMB's value remains primarily
as a **muscle-preservation** tool, and its interaction with ketones is more
about *modulating ketone effects on muscle* than about *generating ketones*.
