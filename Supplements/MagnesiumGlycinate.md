# Magnesium Glycinate

Magnesium glycinate supports sleep through several mechanisms.

## Calms the nervous system  

Magnesium binds to and activates **GABA receptors**, the brain's primary 
inhibitory (calming) neurotransmitter, while also blocking NMDA receptors to 
dampen excitatory glutamate signaling. This helps "turn off" racing thoughts at 
night.

## Supports Melatonin Production  

Magnesium is a cofactor for the enzymes that convert serotonin into melatonin, 
the hormone that regulates your sleep-wake cycle. It also helps lower cortisol, 
the stress hormone that fragments sleep when elevated at night.

## Relaxes Muscles  

Magnesium opposes calcium's contraction signal in muscles, easing tension, 
cramps, and restless leg symptoms that can disrupt sleep.

## Glycine  

**Glycine adds its own benefit.** The glycine bound to the magnesium is itself
an inhibitory neurotransmitter that lowers core body temperature and promotes
deeper, more restorative sleep stages. It also improves the mineral's absorption
compared to other forms (oxide, citrate), and it's gentle on the stomach.

## Scientific Evidence  

**What the evidence says:** A 2025 randomized trial of 155 adults found **modest
but statistically significant** improvements in insomnia severity after 28 days.
A 2021 meta-analysis of three random control trials found magnesium users fell 
asleep ~17 minutes faster and slept ~16 minutes longer, though researchers 
rated overall evidence quality as **low**. Cleveland Clinic notes the evidence 
is "thin" but acknowledges some people do find it helpful.

## Dosage  

**Typical dose:** 200–400 mg of elemental magnesium, taken **30–60 minutes
before bed**. Benefits are generally gradual (1 to 3 weeks) rather than an
immediate sedative effect.

## Signs of Overdose

Signs of taking too much magnesium glycinate progress from mild digestive upset
to potentially serious systemic effects:

**Early / mild signs** (most common, appear within 30 min to a few hours):

- **Diarrhea** — the body's first and most reliable warning sign that it's
  exceeded its absorption capacity
- Nausea and vomiting
- Abdominal cramping or bloating
- Facial flushing
- Mild lethargy or drowsiness

**Moderate to severe signs** (hypermagnesemia):

- Low blood pressure (dizziness, lightheadedness, fainting)
- Muscle weakness, reduced reflexes, loss of coordination
- Confusion or mental clouding
- Urine retention
- Slow or irregular heartbeat (bradycardia, arrhythmias)
- Difficulty breathing

**Life-threatening** (extremely rare from supplements alone; more likely with
kidney disease):

- Respiratory failure
- Cardiac arrest

**Key safety context:**

- The **tolerable upper intake level** for supplemental magnesium is **350
  mg/day** for adults (this excludes dietary magnesium).
- True toxicity (hypermagnesemia) is **very rare** in healthy people because
  kidneys efficiently excrete excess magnesium. Risk rises significantly with
  **kidney impairment**.
- The glycinate form is gentler on the gut than oxide or citrate, but the same
  osmotic diarrhea mechanism still applies at high doses.

**What to do:**

- Mild symptoms (loose stools, nausea): stop the supplement, hydrate, and wait. 
  Symptoms typically resolve within a few hours.  
- Severe symptoms (breathing difficulty, slow/irregular heartbeat, extreme
  confusion, fainting): **seek emergency medical care immediately.**
