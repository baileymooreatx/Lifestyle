# Methylated vs. Regular Vitamins

The core difference is **activation**: methylated vitamins are already in their
active, ready-to-use form, while regular (non-methylated) vitamins require your
body to convert them first.

| Nutrient        | Regular form   | Methylated (active) form      |
|-----------------|----------------|-------------------------------|
| Folate (B9)     | Folic acid     | 5-MTHF (methylfolate)         |
| Vitamin B12     | Cyanocobalamin | Methylcobalamin               |
| Vitamin B6      | Pyridoxine HCl | P-5-P (pyridoxal-5-phosphate) |
| Riboflavin (B2) | Riboflavin     | Riboflavin-5-phosphate        |

## Why It Matters  

- **Folic acid** must go through several enzymatic conversion steps in the
  liver before it becomes usable active folate. **5-MTHF** skips those steps
  entirely.
- **Cyanocobalamin** is a synthetic form of B12 that the body must convert to
  methylcobalamin or adenosylcobalamin before it can be used.
- People with **MTHFR gene variants** (affecting a significant portion of the
  population) have reduced ability to perform these conversions, so standard
  forms may be poorly utilized.

## Who Benefits Most from Methylated Forms

- People with MTHFR or other methylation-related genetic variants
- Those with gastrointestinal malabsorption issues
- Older adults (B12 absorption declines with age)
- People experiencing fatigue, brain fog, or mood issues despite adequate diet

**A note on evidence:** While methylated forms are more bioavailable by design,
some research (e.g., from *Health* magazine, 2026) suggests that for healthy
adults without conversion issues, the practical difference may be modest. For
those with known genetic variants or absorption problems, the benefit is more
clear-cut. Methylated supplements also tend to be **more expensive** than
standard formulations.
