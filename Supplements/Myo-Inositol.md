## Myo-Inositol

Myo-inositol is a supplement with essentially zero side effects at 4 g/day, no
meaningful drug interactions, and it works on the *actual insulin signaling
pathway* rather than a downstream proxy. Paired with berberine (AMPK) and
magnesium (receptor kinase cofactor), insulin sensitivity is covered from
three complementary angles. 2022 head-to-head data showing ~2 times the
Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR) improvement over
metformin makes it arguably the highest evidence-per-dollar supplement for
insulin resistance specifically.

## Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance

It's a calculated score (not a separate blood test) derived from two fasting lab
values.

> HOMA-IR = (Fasting Insulin [µIU/mL] × Fasting Glucose [mg/dL]) ÷ 405

**Example:** Fasting insulin 12 µIU/mL, fasting glucose 95 mg/dL → (12 × 95) ÷
405 = **2.8**

**Interpretation:**

| Score   | Meaning                                                     |
|---------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| < 1.0   | High insulin sensitivity (optimal)                          |
| 1.0–1.9 | Normal / favorable                                          |
| 2.0–2.9 | Early insulin resistance                                    |
| 3.0–4.9 | Elevated insulin resistance (higher cardiometabolic risk)   |
| ≥ 5.0   | Marked insulin resistance (metabolic syndrome / T2DM range) |


### Why It Matters  

It detects insulin resistance **years before** fasting glucose or HbA1c cross 
into the prediabetes/diabetes range. The 405 divisor was calibrated so that a 
perfectly insulin-sensitive person (insulin ~5 µIU/mL, glucose ~90 mg/dL) scores 
approximately 1.0.

### How to Get It  

Ask your doctor to order a **fasting insulin** alongside your routine fasting 
glucose (many standard metabolic panels don't include insulin). Both must come 
from the same 8 to 12 hour fasting draw. Once you have both numbers, the 
calculation is trivial, or your lab may compute it for you.

Tracking HOMA-IR before and after an intervention using (diet + supplements + 
exercise) is the single best way to confirm that 
insulin sensitivity is actually improving, independent of weight loss.

## What Berberine Is

A six-carbon cyclitol (sugar alcohol) that is one of nine inositol stereoisomers
found in the human body. It's the most abundant form and the one with the
strongest metabolic evidence. Found naturally in citrus fruits, beans, brown
rice, nuts, seeds, and whole grains. The body synthesizes it *de novo* from
glucose-6-phosphate, but this pathway is impaired in insulin resistance.
Excess glucose competitively inhibits the sodium/myo-inositol transporter (
SMIT),
creating a vicious cycle of depletion.

The FDA classifies it as **GRAS** (Generally Recognized As Safe), permitting use
in food and even infant formula.

## How it works

| Mechanism                                        | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
|--------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Phosphoinositide cycle restoration** (primary) | Myo-inositol → phosphatidylinositol → IP₃ / PIP₃ → activates the PI3K/AKT/IRS cascade that insulin uses to signal glucose uptake. In insulin resistance, intracellular inositol pools are depleted, so the cascade stalls. Supplementation replenishes the substrate. |
| **Inositol phosphoglycan (IPG) generation**      | IPGs embedded in the cell membrane have **insulin-mimetic activity** — they can directly activate downstream signaling (IRS → AKT → GLUT-4) even without insulin binding, providing a "backup" glucose-uptake signal.                                                 |
| **AMPK activation**                              | Upregulates GLUT-4 protein expression and its translocation to the plasma membrane. Confirmed in human endometrial cells under insulin-resistant conditions (2020, *Am J Physiol-Endocrinol Metab*).                                                                  |
| **GLUT-4 translocation**                         | Directly enhances glucose transporter movement to the cell surface, independent of insulin stimulation.                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Anti-oxidant / membrane stabilization**        | Protects cell membranes from lipid peroxidation; reduces hepatic triglyceride accumulation (hepatoprotective in NAFLD models).                                                                                                                                        |
| **Gut–liver axis modulation**                    | Emerging evidence for modulation of gut-liver signaling that reduces hepatic steatosis.                                                                                                                                                                               |

## Clinical Evidence

| Outcome                      | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
|------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **HOMA-IR**                  | 2018 meta-analysis (*Eur Rev Med Pharmacol Sci*): significant reduction, driven by improved insulin sensitivity **independent of weight loss**. A 2022 *Open Heart* review found myo-inositol improves insulin resistance **~2× better than metformin** on HOMA-IR. |
| **Fasting insulin**          | 12-week RCT in PCOS: 2 g BID → significant reductions in plasma insulin, LH, testosterone, and LH:FSH ratio.                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Fasting glucose**          | 2016 RCT in T2DM: 3 months of supplementation → significant HbA1c and fasting glucose reduction.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Lipids**                   | 2 g BID → triglycerides −20%, HDL +22%, diastolic BP −11% (metabolic syndrome cohort).                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Gestational diabetes**     | 2 g BID → significant improvement in fasting glucose and 1-hour OGTT glycemia.                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| **NAFLD**                    | 2024 RCT (*PMC*): 4 g/day → enhanced AKT and PDK-1 expression in PBMCs, improved AMPK/PI3K/AKT pathway, reduced hepatic steatosis markers.                                                                                                                          |
| **Anxiety / Panic disorder** | 12–18 g/day → reduced panic attack frequency comparable to fluvoxamine (1995 trial); mixed results for depression.                                                                                                                                                  |

## Dosage  

| Use                                         | Dose                                      | Timing                                   |
|---------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Insulin resistance / metabolic syndrome** | 2–4 g/day (split into 2 doses of 1–2 g)   | **AM and PM with meals**                 |
| **PCOS**                                    | 4 g/day (2 g × 2)                         | **AM and PM** (AM dose before breakfast) |
| **NAFLD**                                   | 4 g/day (single dose)                     | **With a meal**                          |
| **Anxiety / OCD** (clinical use only)       | 12–18 g/day (split × 3)                   | **TID**                                  |
| **Starting out**                            | 1 g/day for the first week, then increase | **With meals**                           |

## Side Effects  

At the standard 2–4 g/day dose, the safety profile is **comparable to placebo**.
A 2011 safety review of >20 clinical trials found:

- **No side effects** at 4 g/day
- **Mild GI symptoms** (nausea, bloating, gas, loose stools) only at **≥ 12
  g/day**
- No kidney, hepatic, or cognitive toxicity identified at any tested dose
- Doses up to **18 g/day for 3 months** and **4 g/day for 1 year** studied
  without serious adverse effects

## Drug interactions

| Medication                                        | Interaction                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|---------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Metformin**                                     | Additive glucose-lowering; one study showed more pronounced drops in blood sugar, insulin resistance markers, and hormone levels when combined. Generally safe but monitor for hypoglycemia. |
| **Lithium**                                       | Theoretical concern — inositol can antagonize lithium's effect on second-messenger systems. Not well-studied clinically; avoid without psychiatric supervision.                              |
| **Laxatives / diuretics**                         | May increase GI side effects.                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Diabetes medications** (insulin, sulfonylureas) | Additive hypoglycemic effect — monitor blood glucose.                                                                                                                                        |

## Avoid or Use Caution  

- **Lithium therapy** — potential antagonism (theoretical, but not worth the
  risk)
- **Pregnancy** — generally considered safe (used in GDM trials), but confirm
  with OB
- **Bipolar disorder** — theoretical risk of switching episodes (same concern as
  lithium antagonism)

## Practical Notes  

- **Bioavailability is high** — no food-dependent absorption issue, but taking 
  with meals improves GI tolerance
- **Onset:** Metabolic markers (fasting insulin, HOMA-IR) can shift within 
  **4 to 8 weeks**; full effects typically by **12 weeks**
- **It's an adjunct**, not a replacement for caloric deficit, protein intake,
  and resistance training
- In the US it's sold as a dietary supplement; in some European countries it's
  available as a prescription medication
- **Quality matters:** Look for pure myo-inositol powder or capsules without
  fillers; the powder mixes easily into water or smoothies and has a mild sweet
  taste
